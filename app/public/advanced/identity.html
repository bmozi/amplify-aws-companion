Give each person and tool the right key

Explain the three identities in this app and design a deployment identity that does not rely on root.

Your outcome

Explain the three identities in this app and design a deployment identity that does not rely on root.

Suggested time: 35–50 minutes. Read-only exercise; identity setup is a separate account change.

Imagine a school. A student can open their locker. A teacher can enter a classroom. A maintenance worker can change the locks. Giving every person the master key would make daily work easy, but a lost key could affect the whole building.

Our app has three different kinds of identity. The app user signs in through Cognito. The runtime role lets a service such as Lambda perform its assigned work. The deployment identity creates and changes AWS resources. Signing into the notebook does not give you permission to deploy a new database.

Investigate the current arrangement

  1. With an authorized, temporary non-root AWS session, run the read-only identity check below after replacing YOUR_PROFILE with your own local profile name. Record the account and kind of ARN returned. An ARN is AWS's full resource address. If no scoped session is available, leave the live step unverified and continue with the source review.
  2. Open app/amplify/data/resource.ts. Find allow.owner() and allow.authenticated(). Explain why private notes and the shared word counter use different rules.
  3. Open app/amplify/storage/resource.ts. Trace the identity-based file path. The browser's file-size check does not replace the server's permission checks.
  4. In IAM, inspect the CDK bootstrap roles without editing them. Find the trust policy (who may use the role) and permission policies (what it may do). Record both as separate answers.
aws sts get-caller-identity --profile YOUR_PROFILE

A human sign-in and a GitHub publication job need different authority. A GitHub publisher and a backend deployer follow different role paths. A narrowly scoped frontend publisher role does not automatically limit the CloudFormation execution role that can change backend resources. Read the trust policy and every downstream role before calling either route narrow. Never switch to root because a scoped action is denied.

Design the next configuration

Write a four-row permission table: reader, app user, deployer, and service runtime. For each, name the resources it needs and one action it must be denied. Prefer federated temporary sessions for people and roles for services. The exact federation setup depends on this account's organization and identity provider; do not invent a universal policy that claims to cover every Amplify deployment.

When implementing a human route, configure a separate temporary-session profile, verify its account and ARN, and test a permitted read and an intended denial. Inspect any helper that chooses a source profile; the companion includes scripts/aws_session.py and scripts/credential-process.py. A publishing workflow needs its own role and target review. A disabled release switch controls only the workflow that reads it; it does not revoke the AWS role or stop another workflow that can assume it. Test the practice environment from Lesson 9 only after a current whole-run estimate, chosen spending limit, reserve and cleanup plan pass. Keep a tested administrative recovery route while changing identity configuration.

Prove the boundary

Use two app accounts. Create a private note as A. Confirm B cannot list or change it, including through the API. A hidden Edit button alone is not proof. Keep tokens, passwords, and complete private records out of the evidence file.

Why can a signed-in person still get AccessDenied?

Sign-in establishes who they are. Authorization decides whether that identity may perform this particular action on this particular resource. Investigate the policy and requested resource; granting administrator permission would hide the mistake.

Cost and reset

The inspection creates no new workloads. Temporary app test accounts can be deleted after their records and files are removed. Keep a tested administrative route when changing identity configuration.

Use AI as your engineering partner

Give Codex the relevant source files and sanitized evidence. Ask: “Trace the difference between the deployment role, Lambda execution role and signed-in customer. Find one permission that each should not have. Do not deploy or expand permissions.”

Verify: Check the actual policy and a denied operation; a diagram alone is not proof. Explain the result in your own words and name one condition that would change it. Set a spending and action boundary before permitting cloud work.

Evidence to collect

  • Identify app, runtime, and deployment identities without confusing them.
  • Propose one allowed and one denied operation for each role; record observed results only for roles you can test with authority.
  • Explain why a trust policy and a permission policy answer different questions.

Use the source bundle’s examples/advanced/evidence-template.json. Keep credentials and private data out of shared results.
