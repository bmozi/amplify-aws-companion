Prove you can operate, recover, and hand off the app

Produce evidence that another person can deploy, diagnose, and retire the environment.

Advanced courseFoundation lessonHands-on lab
Your outcome

Produce evidence that another person can deploy, diagnose, and retire the environment.

Suggested time: 90–120 minutes. Capstone exercise; destructive tests only in disposable practice.

A working demonstration is a beginning. A cloud engineer also needs to answer: Which version is running? Who may change it? What happens when a service fails? What survives deletion? Can someone else continue the work?

Write the operating agreement

Choose a small scenario: two learners keep private notes, attach a file, and use one custom function. Define the permissions, an acceptable recovery time, acceptable data loss, and a spending review schedule. Recovery time objective (RTO) is how quickly you intend to recover. Recovery point objective (RPO) describes how much recent data you can tolerate losing. Both are targets until tested.

Run a recovery drill using invented data

  1. Create three known practice notes and a tiny attachment. Record their expected content privately.
  2. Choose a recovery method appropriate to the exercise. For this small drill, a controlled application-level export and re-import can demonstrate recovery of content. Record that it may change record IDs, timestamps, and ownership unless deliberately preserved.
  3. Store the export outside the practice environment being tested. Verify that it can be read before deleting any disposable test records.
  4. Remove only those known test records, then restore through the authenticated application/API. Compare content and owner access with the original expectations.
  5. Record elapsed restoration time and missing information. A file that exists but has never been restored is an untested backup.

For a more advanced infrastructure drill, investigate DynamoDB point-in-time recovery, S3 version recovery, and Cognito's different recovery constraints separately. Database restoration does not automatically reconnect an Amplify API to the restored table. Terraform state is a resource-management record, not a backup of app data. Design and test the reconnection and identity story before relying on a production recovery claim.

Explain the bill with a model

List the things you consume: build minutes, requests, function runtime, stored bytes, and data transfer. Enter current rates from each service's pricing page in your worksheet and label the region, date, and units. Multiply a small expected usage by those rates, then compare with delayed billing data. Free allowances and credits may change the amount owed without removing the underlying usage.

Perform the handoff

  1. Record the source revision or archive hash, tool versions, account, region, resource owners, environment names, and generated-output locations.
  2. Repeat a permitted action and a denied action for two users, plus one failure/recovery case.
  3. Have another person follow the playbook if available. Record their actual questions and results. Your own successful run does not establish that every reader understands it.
  4. Delete the disposable practice environment, then inspect retained storage, logs, snapshots, and other support resources. Mark each retained item intentionally kept or still needing attention.

Your completion evidence

Fill in examples/advanced/evidence-template.json for each exercise and keep results privately under evidence/live/. Use four distinct statuses: designed, deployed, behavior verified, removed. A checked reading box in this course does not advance a cloud environment through those states.

Explain it back

“Our page is delivered by Hosting. Cognito identifies the user. AppSync applies the API rules. DynamoDB remembers records; S3 stores files; Lambda runs our custom action. Terraform owns Hosting and Amplify owns the backend. We test permissions, preserve evidence, and verify cleanup.” Add the concrete identifiers and observations from your own run.

Use AI as your engineering partner

Give Codex the relevant source files and sanitized evidence. Ask: “Act as a reviewer of this runbook. Ask for evidence of deployment, denial, recovery, cleanup and remaining costs. Do not deploy or expand permissions.”

Verify: Have another person reproduce the procedure; an AI review does not establish learner comprehension. Explain the result in your own words and name one condition that would change it. Set a spending and action boundary before permitting cloud work.

Evidence to collect

  • Recover invented data and compare content plus ownership.
  • Document measured recovery time and unresolved gaps.
  • Complete a source-to-resource handoff and verify practice cleanup.

Use the source bundle’s examples/advanced/evidence-template.json. Keep credentials and private data out of shared results.
