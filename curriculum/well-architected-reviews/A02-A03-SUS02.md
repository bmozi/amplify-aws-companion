# SUS 02: match capacity to the promise

Reviewed October 19, 2026 against the six [AWS Well-Architected SUS 02 demand-alignment practices](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/framework/sus-02.html). This is a teaching and design review of A02 repair processing and A03 seat reservations, not a Well-Architected Tool finding, AWS benchmark, account cost estimate, environmental measurement or production service-level agreement. The [A02/A03 Terraform](../../architectures/reliable-workflows/infra/main.tf) declares an SQS work queue and a Lambda mapping with maximum concurrency two; the [local worker-capacity model](../../examples/demand-alignment/README.md) deliberately uses fictional one-minute jobs-per-worker rates. It does not emulate Lambda or SQS.

## First decide when the customer needs the result

Maya's desk can say “your repair request is accepted” before the repair is finished **only** if that accepted request is durable and recoverable. The processing deadline is a second promise. Seat confirmation is different: the last seat must be assigned by an authoritative conditional decision before either customer gets a confirmed answer. Buffering that final decision would change the transaction, not merely save capacity.

![A burst of 90 fictional repair jobs enters a durable-acceptance boundary, then three worker schedules are compared at the minute-four completion deadline. The smallest worker-minute number misses 30 jobs; the scaled schedule meets the nominal deadline but has limited interruption margin.](../../book/figures/demand-promise-envelope.svg)

The [local worksheet](../../examples/demand-alignment/README.md) holds the synthetic workload and the minute-four deadline fixed. Four workers for eight minutes: 32 provisioned worker-minutes, all 90 on time. Two workers for four minutes: eight worker-minutes, all 90 on time. One worker for six minutes: six worker-minutes, only 60 on time. The last option makes one meter smaller by making the customer outcome worse. None of these values is an AWS duration, energy reading, price or carbon estimate. They omit queue polling, storage, reads, retries, logs, client work and recovery capacity.

## Changed-condition exercise: the cheap schedule breaks

The fictional baseline has 90 repairs ready at minute zero and a minute-four deadline. A teammate recommends **one worker for six minutes**, because six provisioned worker-minutes are less than the two-worker plan's eight. Run the local model and decide. Your answer must state the timely/late counts, customer promise, missing cost/resource meters, and whether there is any measured environmental conclusion. Then the intake grows to **120 jobs** at minute zero. The two-worker plan completes all 120 exactly at minute four. A worker becomes unavailable during minute three. What happens to the deadline and which observation or recovery reserve would you require before choosing capacity?

A second teammate proposes telling customers “accepted” before an SQS send succeeds, or throttling a kiosk that cannot retry. Explain what receipt or refusal the customer would actually get. The last seat remains one: would you put its conditional assignment behind the same queue? State the changed business contract if you did. Ask Codex to challenge your answer with a missed-deadline or lost-acknowledgement counterexample; check its arithmetic with the local model and its AWS suggestions against the [current AWS practices](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/framework/sus-02.html) and checked-in Terraform. Write your first decision before reading the [assessor](A02-A03-SUS02-ASSESSOR.md).

**Review disposition:** all six SUS 02 practices now have a workload-specific teaching decision. The local model demonstrates a deadline/capacity counterexample under stated assumptions; it does not prove the SQS acknowledgement boundary, Lambda service behavior, selected scaling policy, full cost, measured energy/carbon, independent learner transfer or exact-final book review. A later live test needs current plan and credit terms and limits chosen for the account; none was performed here.
