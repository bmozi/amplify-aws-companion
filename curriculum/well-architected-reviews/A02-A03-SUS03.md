Read the [architecture study](../ARCHITECTURE-STUDIES.md#a02-repair-request-processing), [Terraform](../../architectures/reliable-workflows/infra/main.tf), [hold-transition exercise](../../examples/reservation-cloud-transition/README.md), [cost packet](../../examples/costs/A02-A03-BOUNDED-RUN.md) and [resource-intensity drill](../../examples/costs/resource-intensity.py) as separate kinds of evidence.

## The repair queue and the last seat are different promises

Maya can tell a repair customer “we accepted your request and will process it soon.” A queue lets the front desk finish quickly while workers process a burst later. She cannot tell two customers “you both have the last seat.” The seat decision needs an authoritative conditional write or transaction before confirmation. Calling both jobs “asynchronous” would hide that difference.

Resource efficiency also has more than one meter. Shorter worker duration may reduce one compute proxy while extra polling, index writes, storage, retries or client downloads increase other work. The right denominator is **correct, timely customer outcomes**, not attempts or successful-looking responses. The local [resource-intensity exercise](../../examples/costs/SUSTAINABILITY-REVIEW.md#count-accepted-outputs-from-attempt-records) demonstrates a deceptively lower worker-seconds-per-output ratio that misses a required output. It measures fictional worker time only; it cannot infer electricity or emissions.

## Changed-condition exercise: the one due hold

There are ten synthetic holds in a table with many unrelated records. One hold becomes due. A teammate says: “The GSI query returns fewer rows, so it is greener and cheaper; remove the scan immediately.” Another says: “SQS means the workers use no resources while idle.” A third wants to shorten the browser polling interval so customers see seat status sooner. The business still requires a correct last-seat decision and an expiry process that eventually finds every due hold.

Before reading the [assessor](A02-A03-SUS03-ASSESSOR.md), write a decision with four parts: **customer promise**, **whole-path resource inventory**, **local or cloud observation needed**, and **unresolved environmental claim**. Explain why each teammate's statement is too strong. Then change the facts: the table grows tenfold and holds arrive throughout the day. Which option becomes more plausible, and which authority and index-lag checks still survive the change? Use Codex to propose a counterexample with a missed candidate or late release; verify it against the source and local tests rather than accepting an elegant diagram.
