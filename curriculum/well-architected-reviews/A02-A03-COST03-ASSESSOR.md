Read the [learner case](A02-A03-COST03.md#changed-condition-exercise-the-green-chart-and-the-missing-repairs) only after attempting it. This is author-written reasoning over fictional rows, not a scored learner result or account observation.

| Baseline | $78 | $12 | $11 | $101 | −$10 | $91 |
| Current | $78 | $12 | $27 | $117 | −$15 | $102 |

The $16 pre-credit increase is the fictional internet-out line growing from $2 to $18 as its quantity grows from 20 to 180 GB. Other listed rows stay flat. The `Network` label is a fixture category, not a selected AWS transfer meter; a real route needs origin, destination, direction, service and time before pricing or assigning an owner. The increased credit changes the after-credit figure but does not reduce resource consumption.

Using only the **directly repair-tagged** subtotal, the baseline proxy is `$78 ÷ 1,000 = $0.078` and current is `$78 ÷ 800 = $0.0975` per correct on-time repair. The flat project chart hides a 25% rise in this narrow ratio because fewer qualifying outcomes arrived. The proxy omits the shared platform, unallocated network/storage/logs and any unsupported tags. The full-account ratios `$101 ÷ 1,000 = $0.101` and `$117 ÷ 800 = $0.14625` can be computed but are not established repair unit costs: the whole account may serve other work. Dividing after-credit totals would answer a different payment question and would still lack allocation evidence.

A supported shared-platform rule might use measured compute/request consumption by workload over the same month, if those metrics really drive the shared bill. An equal split is a declared accounting convention, not evidence of equal use. The growing network line needs a path and owner investigation first; if Maya cannot establish what sent the bytes, keep it unallocated and report the unresolved $27 current bucket. A tag added after the fact should not be assumed to rewrite earlier billing rows without a documented, verified backfill path. Reconcile any proposed workload allocations back to the account pre-credit total and label what remains shared or unknown.

The day-28 worker peak and missed database p99 make an average-utilization resize unsafe without representative close and recovery tests. An alert can trigger investigation after data arrives; it cannot prevent a deadline miss or guarantee the credit reserve. A discount changes payment terms, not accepted repairs. Require the same correct-on-time identity set or an explicitly approved changed promise, peak and failure performance, complete cost scope, and a tested rollback before declaring improvement.

With zero correct on-time repairs, cost per qualifying repair is **undefined**, never `$0` or an infinite-price estimate presented as a measured unit price. The charges still exist and the customer promise failed. A learner who only says “the repair chart stayed at $78” has not joined cost to outcomes. A learner who allocates all untagged or credited dollars to repair without evidence has hidden the central attribution gap. The two local scripts verify fictional bill and declared-summary arithmetic, including a zero-denominator boundary; two aggregate outcome rows cannot verify distinct customer receipts, correctness, deadlines, AWS behavior, current prices or reader mastery.
