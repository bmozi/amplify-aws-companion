# Assessor: SUS 02 deadline and capacity case

Read the [case](A02-A03-SUS02.md#changed-condition-exercise-the-cheap-schedule-breaks) after making an independent decision. This is authored reasoning, not a learner result, AWS benchmark or environmental estimate.

The one-worker plan completes 15 fictional jobs per minute. By the end of minute four it has completed 60 of 90; 30 finish in minutes five and six. Six provisioned worker-minutes is therefore **not** a comparable improvement under the unchanged promise. The two-worker plan supplies 30 jobs per minute and nominally finishes 90 after minute three; leaving it on through minute four uses eight provisioned worker-minutes and leaves one nominal minute of room. The four-worker all-eight-minutes plan has 32 provisioned worker-minutes. These are schedule counts, not Lambda billing or energy.

At 120 jobs, two workers require all four minutes. Removing one worker for minute three removes capacity for 15 jobs. Only 105 are complete at the deadline; 15 remain. The decision needs a bounded burst profile, recovery target, queue age and worker-duration evidence. Additional capacity may be justified by the business promise, but size it using the selected failure case and complete cost estimate. Do not call the option “green” from the provisioned worker-minute comparison alone: queue requests and polling, table reads/writes, retries, logs, storage, client work and real service behavior remain outside the model.

“Accepted” should mean an authoritative accepted record or durable queue handoff with a recoverable status, not an optimistic UI acknowledgement before the required write. If the queue send or receipt is uncertain, recover by the stable request identity before promising success or asking for an unsafe retry. A kiosk unable to retry cannot be throttled with an invisible failure. It needs a bounded buffer, an explicit refusal, or a changed client contract approved by the business. A03's last-seat confirmation requires a conditional authoritative decision before confirmation; using the repair queue for that final choice would turn immediate confirmation into pending status and introduce duplicate/stale decision hazards.

A strong answer compares **correct and timely outputs for the same load**, describes both nominal and interrupted paths, and marks unknown AWS behavior and cost. It distinguishes reducing unnecessary provisioned capacity from measured electricity or emissions. No independent learner has been assessed with this packet.
