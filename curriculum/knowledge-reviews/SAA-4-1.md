## Why these mappings remain partial

The cost objective requires more than identifying a compatible service. The manuscript names the relevant bill components, and now walks a reader through an explicitly simplified fictional storage cost comparison in which access frequency, object size and early deletion change the preferred choice. That was the substantive teaching gap identified by this review. The subsequent [worked comparison and local exercise](../../examples/storage-cost/README.md) now cover fictional retrieval, early deletion, per-object floors and deadline exclusion, with separate assessor reasoning. G05 retains real workload pricing and observed behavior as open conditions. The new lesson first rejects choices that miss the interface or delivery requirement, then compares the included cost components over the same period using explicit fictional inputs. Completing the real comparison still requires dated regional prices and omitted charges. It must not present an archive's lower stored-byte rate as the entire decision.

A gateway cache hit does not demonstrate offline working-set availability. Correct backlog arithmetic does not establish gateway performance. An authored restore deadline does not prove service recovery or permission enforcement. Reusing relevant teaching across performance and cost objectives is justified only with these different remaining conditions recorded.
