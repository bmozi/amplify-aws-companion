# Keep the mechanism after closing the book

Use these original cases without notes, AI or the answer guide during the first attempt. Draw the path, make a decision and explain why a credible alternative fails. Record uncertainty. Use the [learning record](LEARNING-RECORD.md) for the first answer and a separate open-book revision.

At the next session, spend a few minutes reconstructing the previous lesson's mechanism. At each stage boundary, attempt the case below and one earlier case whose reasoning you found difficult. After a longer gap, attempt its changed condition without your old answer visible. Next session, about a week and about a month are adjustable reminders, not optimal intervals established for every reader. If you remember the exact answer, invent a new requirement or ask a tutor to supply an unfamiliar case after your unaided attempt.

## R1 — After Chapters 1–5

A repair request returns a timeout. A log says its acceptance record exists; no appointment has been assigned. Someone proposes a new request ID and twice as many workers. State what the customer can be told, what should happen before retrying, and which measurement would justify more workers. Now the log cannot be matched to this customer: which conclusion must you withdraw?

## R2 — After Chapters 6–10

A preview page shows the correct new heading. Its sign-in configuration matches development, but its data endpoint matches production. Bob's note list is empty. Decide whether testing can continue with private notes and which destination and access observations are missing. Now the preview must deliberately share a test backend: what changes in your acceptance criteria?

## R3 — After Chapters 11–13

A booking commits successfully. The confirmation screen reads a replica one minute behind. A proposed cache makes that screen faster. Choose the read contract for confirmation and for a report allowed to lag two minutes. Then both workers attempt the last slot: where must that decision be enforced, and why does a fresh read alone not enforce it?

## R4 — After Chapters 14–18

After a release, the page remains healthy but accepted work misses its deadline. A standby can answer HTTP, yet its recovery role cannot decrypt the backup. Compare rollback and restore as possible responses: what observation would justify each, what would stop it, and how would you verify useful service? Now old clients continue writing during rollback: name the compatibility question that becomes decisive.

## R5 — After Chapters 19–25

A payment provider accepts an operation but drops its reply. The worker restarts, the approval has expired, and the report still shows unpaid. Explain which actions require new authority and which reconcile an existing effect. Specify how a stable operation identity and provider evidence affect the next step. Now two workers see the same pending record: what must coordinate them?

## R6 — After Chapters 26–31

A second shop is migrated to a shared platform. Its private network route works; a document index still contains old access metadata, and the target Region's quota fits normal traffic but not overlapping old and new workers. Name three separate readiness checks and a reason to hold cutover. Now the index is rebuilt: what must still be tested before releasing a retrieved answer?

## R7 — After Chapters 32–34

Two designs meet ordinary traffic demand. A costs 80 fictional units monthly and recovers in 40 minutes. B costs 120 and recovers in 8 minutes. Both preserve at most 2 minutes of data loss. The business requires a 10-minute RTO and 5-minute RPO. Choose under the supplied assumptions, identify missing operational evidence, and explain why a weighted preference score cannot rescue a failed requirement. Now the RTO becomes 60 minutes: what must you compare again?

## Check, repair and return

Only after saving the attempt, open [the separate reasoning guide](assessor/RETRIEVAL-PRACTICE-KEY.md). Mark each mechanism supported, incomplete or contradicted. Revisit the named chapter for the exact misconception, then solve a changed case without copying the explanation. A correct familiar answer does not establish independent transfer. These cases assess reasoning; they do not test deployed AWS controls or predict an exam result.
