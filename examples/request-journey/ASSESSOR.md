# Assessor notes for the customer request journey

Judge the learner's explanation of the **business promise**, not whether it repeats the JSON labels. A good answer distinguishes a durable receipt, dispatch, a queue delivery, a completed simulated appointment and an actual external supplier booking. These are five different facts.

For the four-hour outage case, the request and outbox records preserve the accepted work. The request stores a server-sampled `accepted_at` second that is retained on retry, and the status read lets Alice see `received_processing`. The A02 domain has manual Moto-tested contact and overdue-worklist methods. A private, default-off Terraform candidate now adds an operator-invoked handler for only the due check and open-worklist read; it does not record contact, schedule a check, deliver an alert, notify a customer or assign an owner. The candidate has not been deployed. The fixture's contact time does not prove a real person reached Alice. A learner should propose an operational deadline process based on acceptance time and distinguish each stored fact from customer evidence. A queue-depth alarm alone could miss a request still in the outbox or a message already lost to retention.

The separate SQLite contact-clock exercise **does** record an immutable local acceptance second and a first-contact second. At the inclusive deadline (3,600 seconds after acceptance), a recorded contact meets the fictional promise. The sample check at 3,601 creates one escalation row; retry and repeated checks cannot reset the clock or duplicate that row. The check's return value is only *new* work. If its reply is lost, a later check returns no new row, but `read_open_worklist` still finds the committed open item. Twenty-five open items require two pages with the supplied limit. Recording contact removes an item from the open view while preserving the historical escalation; a first contact at hour four is still late. Neither an empty new-work response nor an empty page after one cursor proves that every obligation was handled. The checker does not send an alert or make the contact, and a delayed or unrun check cannot establish timely human response. A02's new manual Moto path models the same fact split in DynamoDB items; neither path supplies a deployed customer route or automatic monitoring.

The [pre-deadline continuation](README.md#look-ahead-before-the-one-hour-promise) corrects the decision timing, not the contact evidence. A watchlist at second 2,700 gives an invented 900-second lead. At second 3,600 an uncontacted item is still due soon under the inclusive rule; at 3,601 it is overdue. A same-ID acceptance retry keeps the original deadline. A five-minute lead cannot satisfy a ten-minute investigation-and-contact path even when the checker runs perfectly. The 25-item fixture needs two pages; interruption after page one leaves the old completion receipt in place, and `sweep_health` later marks it stale. A subsequent terminal-cursor walk writes a new local receipt. But an earlier-deadline request inserted after page one sorts behind the cursor, so that completed pass records 25 items while a fresh pass finds 26. A completion receipt proves a bounded traversal, not a concurrent snapshot or the correctness of the worklist. A missing or old supplied receipt should trigger investigation of the checker itself; `recent` says only that a supplied completion time falls within the chosen gap, not that an alert reached anyone or a customer was contacted. Ask for the scheduler run, all cursors, reconciliation, delivery, pickup and customer-outcome evidence separately before claiming a live promise.

For the [repeat-pass comparison](README.md#repeat-the-watchlist-from-the-beginning), the first complete local walk can miss a request placed behind its cursor; the restarted walk identifies that request as `repeat_only`. This is a useful *detected difference*, not a proof that the repeat saw everything. Compare IDs rather than only the total. If R27 arrives behind the second cursor, the two observed sets may agree while R27 is still absent. An assessor should reject “two matching passes mean all customers are safe,” “the fresh receipt proves the checker was scheduled,” and “a found ticket proves a human contacted Maya.” The next design decision must name an independently durable acceptance source, scan/reconciliation cadence, alert delivery and operator ownership, deadline margin and whole-path read/compute/log cost. The SQLite fixture tests none of those live controls.

The [operator-claim continuation](README.md#claiming-an-overdue-ticket-is-not-contact) adds a local ownership fact, not verified contact. A same-operator claim retry returns the first generation while active; a rival is refused. Once a later claim takes the expired item, the former generation cannot write the first-contact attestation. The item remains open until a matching live claim records one fixture timestamp. A lost attestation reply may be retried with the same operator, generation and contact second even after the lease expires; a changed contact second cannot rewrite the first result. Reject any claim that the stale-write refusal proves Operator A never phoned Maya, or that a claimed ticket met the one-hour promise. The replacement needs out-of-band outcome reconciliation before another call. These tests use trusted source-visible names and timestamps in one SQLite store, not real operator authentication, a distributed lease, customer confirmation or AWS evidence.

Accept a design that keeps the receipt truthful and adds a reviewed escalation process with a measured deadline. For the 25-request changed case, require the learner to follow both scan cursor chains and recover open work after the due-check reply is lost. The mock-provider Terraform test establishes only default-off and source wiring; the Moto test establishes only emulated handler behavior. Reject an answer that changes the screen to “booked” merely to reduce anxiety, treats an empty queue or an empty newly-opened page as proof of completion, or claims the local fixture proved live AWS identity or supplier idempotency. The supplier's operation ID and authoritative lookup must be defined before automatic replay after an ambiguous reply.

For the privacy variant, ask which request fields must reach the overdue checker. The correct answer names the key and acceptance time for discovery, plus escalation/contact state for the worklist. The new projections keep the invented request description out of these local responses, but they do not make the role field-scoped or reduce DynamoDB scan read capacity. The learner should retain the full-item-size cost assumption and require live policy review before calling the boundary enforced in AWS.

The learner should compare at least two feasible responses. A synchronous supplier call before returning a receipt may avoid a long pending state when the supplier is healthy, but during a four-hour outage it cannot satisfy quick durable acceptance without another fallback. Durable acceptance plus later scheduling preserves the request, but carries a status, dispatch, escalation, retention and cost obligation. The business must decide whether one-hour contact is required and fund the observation/recovery path.

Evidence to request: the actual local JSON; the A02 `submit`, `dispatch`, `process` and status-route code; a test of a missing or altered retry identity; an age/deadline scenario; and, only for a later cloud claim, scoped identity, AWS read-back, a failure drill, customer-visible timing, attributed cost and verified teardown. An AI-generated design critique is useful review input, not independent learner validation.
