# Assessor notes — S3 upload strategy

- Keep customer photographs as separate objects when each needs its own identity, ownership checks, update/deletion path or retention decision. Batching the client's work can mean bounded concurrent uploads while preserving one object per photograph; it need not mean combining their bytes into one archive.
- Device readings are candidates for aggregation when the query pattern is grouped, the two-minute freshness allowance is real, and the partition/index format supports the needed queries. Firehose buffering is one possible design, not an automatic cheapest choice. Include its stream, processing, storage, query, buffering, retry and monitoring costs and limits.
- A 12-GiB file is one logical object, so multipart upload is a candidate for transferring that single object in parts and retrying parts. The upload must be completed; incomplete parts remain until completed or aborted and have their own cleanup/cost implications. Verify the chosen client's checksum and failure behavior.
- For the stated 10,000 readings, one object per reading means 10,000 destination objects and, under the simplified assumption of one PUT per object, 10,000 writes. Grouping 100 records into each of 100 files means 100 destination objects/writes. This does not imply a 100-fold total-cost reduction: storage, read requests, queries, pipeline processing, retention, retries, compression and required latency also matter.
- S3 Batch Operations runs a selected operation over a manifest of existing objects and can report results. It does not upload a new collection of client files as one `PutObject` call.
- A sound design records stable item identities, batch/file manifests, expected counts or ranges, checksums, delivery outcomes and retry identity. A successful transport call alone does not prove that every business record arrived once or can be queried.
- Recheck current S3, Firehose and client-library documentation, feature limits and regional prices before implementation. This paper case is not an AWS benchmark, transfer, delivery guarantee, or learner result.
