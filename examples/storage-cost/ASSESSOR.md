# Provisional storage-cost reasoning

Use only after the learner's unaided attempt. These are authored criteria, not calibrated exam scoring or observed learner performance.

1. Ready costs 100 × 3 × 0.02 = 6 CU. Shelf costs 1.5 CU storage + 0.1 entry + 0.4 retrieval = 2 CU. Both meet the fictional deadline. Identify every term rather than guessing from the rate.
2. Shelf becomes 1.5 + 0.1 + 6 = 7.6 CU; Ready remains 6 in this simplified model. Repeated reads explain cumulative retrieval above stored capacity. Equality is 1.6 + 0.04R = 6, hence R = 110 GiB. Real request and transfer costs can change that boundary.
3. Ready costs two-thirds CU, about 0.67. Shelf still incurs 90 charged days: 1.5 + 0.1 = 1.6 CU. Physical deletion and accrued minimum-duration liability are different facts. Do not suggest retaining data contrary to its required deletion policy merely to use the paid period.
4. Actual content is 1 MiB. Shelf bills 1,024 × 64 KiB = 64 MiB, or 0.0625 GiB. Storage is 0.0009375 CU and entry is 1.024 CU, totaling 1.0249375. Ready costs 0.00005859375 CU. Entry dominates Shelf in this case. Report unrounded terms before rounding tiny totals for presentation.
5. Shelf misses the delivery requirement. It is not an eligible winner even though its arithmetic total is lower. A real proposal needs measured complete delivery behavior, suitable identity controls and recovery evidence, not the fixture's constant timing.

For the packing variation, expect fewer entry operations and potentially less floor overhead, but a changed access unit. Fetching one customer's record might require reading a larger package. Explain indexing, allowed readers, deletion/rewrite implications, failure and retry scope. A packing suggestion without those tradeoffs is incomplete.

The calculator checks arithmetic and rejects selected invalid inputs. It does not validate an actual AWS price, billing rule, lifecycle configuration, retrieval deadline or user understanding. A separately planned cloud experiment needs a complete estimate within the account owner's chosen limit and a chosen credit reserve. Fictional CU are not a spending authorization or current prices.
