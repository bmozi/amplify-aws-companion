# Professional deployment choice: assessor reasoning

These criteria evaluate fictional design reasoning. They are not an AWS rollout, current price quote, IAM simulation, learner score or assurance that a selected service fits an uninspected application.

1. A private credential cannot be put in public browser code. Keeping the existing web delivery path while adding a scoped server-side operation is a plausible low-change option if authorization, durable intent and retries are designed. A whole-service migration requires another need, such as incompatible hosting behavior or operating constraints, and brings a new traffic, data and recovery path. Compare the complete bill and operator responsibilities. A reminder must not double-send merely because an attempt was retried.
2. An urgent in-place action may be justified by a real deadline and known supported repair, but must be recorded, bounded, tested and reconciled into the image source. The replacement route must fix and test the image recipe, agent/start/stop behavior, health and application revision. The previous CodeDeploy stop hook can affect the next deployment; inspect the actual hook and target events. Neither a new AMI nor an emergency patch proves the customer operation or future replacements are safe.
3. The existing App Runner customer may continue operating that service, but AWS has closed it to new customers. Its managed source/image-to-running-service path does not transfer application/dependency compatibility, stored data, customer access, source trust or business recovery to AWS. ECS Express Mode requires a container image and roles, and creates billable underlying ECS/Fargate, load-balancing and networking resources. Compare the exact source-to-image build, runtime, schema overlap, tested customer result and migration/rollback path. The old artifact cannot recover against a column it no longer understands.
4. A change set previews proposed CloudFormation resource actions. It does not establish a safe stateful replacement, restored records, acceptable customer interruption or a working rollback. Hold execution pending owner and data decision, exact changes, supported replacement behavior, tested backup/restore, cost overlap, stop authority and a customer-level probe. IaC review and CI checks are necessary evidence, each with a limited scope.
5. Stop advancing and use the selected controller's tested traffic recovery if the old artifact is still compatible. Reconcile the 100 sent messages by stable effect identifiers and supplier/customer evidence; do not simply replay or erase them. Determine whether new records remain readable by old code. If not, reject an automatic old-code rollback and choose a controlled compatible or roll-forward path. The local canary demonstrates only its loopback route and synthetic outcome; the SQLite lab demonstrates its sequential compatibility fixture, not external side effects or distributed writers.
6. Fargate removes host provisioning for a supported container deployment, not container-image maintenance, task/service definition, traffic health, graceful shutdown, shared storage permission or customer-data recovery. The local Docker drill can show a process's readiness and drain behavior under its fixture. It cannot prove ECS capacity, task scheduling, target-group, EFS or IAM behavior. Compare recurring capacity, overlap and operating effort with the current EC2 path before migrating.

Accept a different platform choice when the learner states the changed requirement and its tradeoffs. Reject “managed” as a synonym for “no operations” and “rollback” as a synonym for “all effects undone.” Independent learner and selected-service implementation evidence remain pending.
