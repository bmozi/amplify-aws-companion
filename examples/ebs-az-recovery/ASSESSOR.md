# Assessor reasoning — EBS zone and recovery clock

This is authored guidance, not a learner result or an AWS disaster-recovery test. Judge the first answer before the Codex-assisted revision.

The existing volume cannot attach to the replacement in another Availability Zone. A completed snapshot can create a new volume in Zone B in the same Region. It is a point-in-time copy, not active replication. In the supplied clock, the snapshot captured at minute 10 and failure occurs at minute 14, so up to four minutes of later writes may be absent. The two-minute RPO is unsupported. The fictional RTO sum is `2 + 3 + 6 + 4 = 15` minutes, exactly the target, leaving no margin and no actual AWS or customer-path measurement. A reader should **hold the business promise** rather than turn arithmetic into a release claim.

The minute-12 snapshot is unavailable until minute 16, after failure, so it cannot be assumed usable at minute 14. A five-minute RPO and 20-minute RTO make the original minute-10 path fit the invented clock, but only as a design candidate needing a drill. Changing first-read initialization from six to twelve minutes yields 21 minutes and rejects the 20-minute RTO even in this model. A fast snapshot restore candidate may address first-read initialization and adds its own enabled snapshot/AZ time cost; it cannot recover writes that occurred after capture or prove application consistency.

For an instance-only failure while the zone and volume survive, a same-zone replacement may use the existing volume after the original attachment state, access, file system and database integrity are resolved. That path might preserve newer writes than a snapshot restore, but the exercise gives no measured restart time or safe reattachment outcome. The strongest answers distinguish the disk's location, snapshot availability, data age, modeled recovery steps and observed customer recovery. They name KMS/IAM and capacity as gates, keep a complete cost and cleanup inventory, and ask for a drilled RPO/RTO result before a firm promise. A diagram or a passed unit test is not a cloud or learner outcome.
