For the variations:

1. The stated checks remove several blockers, but the team still needs its declared workload/capacity, dependencies, monitoring, connection behavior and operating acceptance. Recovery time runs from the explicitly chosen incident start to validated useful service. If that start is 10:01, evidence at 10:15 covers fourteen minutes so far; it does not establish the final RTO until the remaining acceptance conditions pass.
2. B's confirmed 112 makes A stale. Returning traffic can lose visible business history. Reconcile the data and define write ownership before a planned return. Treat failback as another controlled migration. Preserving B temporarily may be safer than rushing toward the originally named primary.
3. Regional replication can carry a logical error. Investigate an appropriate historical recovery point and independent business evidence. Correct replica agreement is not sufficient when both copies agree on a wrong value. External charges or messages may require separate reconciliation.
4. State the missing confirmed record and unresolved fields. Do not invent a booking or claim zero loss from matching only the known subset. Seek authorized independent evidence and an explicit business decision about degraded/read-only service or unmet recovery objectives.
5. Low-volume correctness is not capacity proof. Measure the intended recovery workload within an authorized bound; consider priority/degraded operation if the business permits it. Reopening beyond capacity can create another outage.
6. Recovery access is part of the dependency graph. Design and test a controlled independent access path before an incident, with appropriate authorization, monitoring and restoration of ordinary access afterward. Merely naming an emergency role does not establish that it can be reached or assumed.

Look for an explicit difference between unavailable service and unknown data, between routing and write authority, and between failover and failback. An acceptable alternative design can choose a different recovery strategy if it states the cost, consistency and operating consequences. Do not score service-name recall as evidence that the design meets the requirements.

For transfer, ask what changes if two Regions must accept bookings continuously. The learner must address business invariants and conflict/coordination behavior, not simply add a second active box. Exact service semantics and failure behavior require a separate service-specific review and implementation evidence.

Count DRS per-server replication, staging resources, snapshots and drill/recovery resources. Three small workers total 900 nominal requests/s, but the database caps the simplified path at 700. If two small workers share the failed AZ, only one 300-capacity worker remains, below demand. Four split evenly across two AZs leave 600 after either AZ loss, with zero modeled margin. Verify placement, quota, registration/health, warm-up, database contention and a representative booking load before accepting the design. An EC2 Auto Scaling group replaces according to configured health checks; target readiness and business correctness need separate observation. [Health-check scope](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/autoscaling/ec2/userguide/health-checks-overview.html).
3. With a 450 database ceiling, four or more nominal workers still cannot deliver 600 useful requests/s. The team must change the bottleneck with a verified plan, reduce admitted demand under an authorized degraded-service policy, or acknowledge that the requirement is unmet. Worker count is not database capacity.

A future evaluation needs trusted record reconciliation, source write fencing, key access, capacity and customer checks. Operator approval cannot make missing prerequisites true. The gate proves only its deterministic local policy, not AWS failover.
5. Two matching current copies can share a logical error. Restore the selected earlier point into an isolated target, verify good business state, identify legitimate later changes and external charges/messages, replay or reconcile them without duplicates, then test the customer operation. The extra investigation and replay may exceed the original RTO model; record that explicitly instead of hiding the delay.

Assess the changed condition and evidence, not the service-name choice alone. Independent learner performance, selected-service configuration, measured recovery and cost remain unverified.

## Pinned-client reasoning

The current route's HTTP 200 for booking 112 establishes one successful local customer read from B. The pinned path's HTTP 404 for the same ID establishes a different local answer from A after A returns. A's HTTP 200 for older booking 111 is not proof that A has the full confirmed history. `return_to_source()` refuses the move because source rows do not match the fictional journal, including 112. A correct answer withdraws any claim that all customers recovered or that failback is ready. It names how real clients and old writer paths would be observed and tested before reopening.

Keeping A unavailable can prevent its stale data from being served by this fixture, but stranded pinned clients remain a customer problem. The local bridge is a bounded redirect model: a 307 with `Location: /bookings/112` proves only that its guard permitted this local GET; a client that follows gets B's 200, while one that does not follow remains at 307. Candidate unavailability produces 503, and a still-writable source or unknown booking cannot be presented as a safe bridge. It is not authenticated, and its relative Location targets another path on the same loopback host. A real old host needs a verified target, identity, client behavior, monitoring and cost. The cooperative flag is not a writer fence. Neither the redirect nor one 200 proves DNS transition, client reconnection, cross-Region behavior, RTO/RPO or customer-wide recovery. Reject an answer that treats the two paths as interchangeable or lets A write before reverse reconciliation.
