# Assessor reasoning — EBS performance and cost shape

These are expected answers for the fictional case, not observed learner or AWS results.

1. Gp2 needs `6000 / 3 = 2000 GiB` for 6,000 baseline IOPS. At the exercise's invented USD 0.10/GiB-month, that is USD 200 for 30 days. A 600-GiB volume has 1,800 baseline IOPS and can burst only up to 3,000 under the documented smaller-volume rule; it cannot meet a sustained 6,000-IOPS promise by relying on burst credits. Provisioning 2,000 GiB for 600 GiB of data buys unused capacity to get baseline performance. Verify actual I/O size, workload duration and gp2 behavior before applying this reasoning to a system.
2. Gp3's invented lines are `600 × 0.08 = 48`, `(6000 − 3000) × 0.005 = 15`, and `(200 − 125) × 0.06 = 4.50`, totaling **USD 67.50** for the exercise's 30 days. The separately selected IOPS and throughput must satisfy current gp3 limits and the attached instance. This arithmetic alone does not prove customer latency.
3. The simple first-tier io2 line is `600 × 0.125 + 6000 × 0.065 = USD 465`. It is a comparison input, not a rejection of io2. A measured need for different latency, sustained performance or other service capabilities may change the choice. Current io2 pricing has tiers and the exercise deliberately does not model them above 32,000 IOPS.
4. With a stated 4,000-IOPS instance ceiling, changing only the volume cannot meet the stated 6,000-IOPS need. With a 300-MiB/s sequential requirement, the gp2 maximum throughput is too low; gp3's model can configure the setting if its size/IOPS ratio and actual instance support it. Both cases still need an application benchmark and complete bill. Snapshot storage, fast restore or initialization choices, EC2 instance charges, retained detached volumes, logs and cleanup time can change whole-run cost.

Assess the student's first prediction separately from its Codex-assisted revision. A strong answer distinguishes capacity, small-operation rate, byte throughput, latency and the EC2 ceiling; rejects gp2's burst as sustained capacity; itemizes the fictional cost arithmetic; and says exactly what service and customer-path evidence remains. The code validates a simplified rate card and shape rules, not a selected AWS account, live volume, workload or bill.
