# Assessor notes — NAT gateway selection

The training workload can consider one shared zonal gateway if the stated one-hour egress loss is acceptable and its cross-AZ path/cost is measured. The customer workload's five-minute payment-provider interruption promise makes the single zonal dependency a poor unqualified choice. Per-AZ zonal gateways can keep each AZ's route local and avoid losing all cross-AZ users when one gateway's AZ fails, at the cost of additional gateway hours. A regional NAT gateway is a current candidate where supported; evaluate its current AZ behavior, addresses, routes, service limits and price rather than assuming its one resource ID means no failure behavior or lower price.

Private endpoints can change the API-traffic route only for supported services and correctly configured endpoints. They do not replace public egress for the payment provider. If AWS API traffic is 95% of bytes, compare endpoint cost and route behavior to sending those bytes through NAT. If a fixed external IP is required, check the candidate mode's address behavior and actual egress identity before promising an allowlist.

Use equivalent traffic, Regions, AZs and availability promises for the cost comparison. Include NAT gateway-hour and data-processing charges, cross-AZ transfer, endpoint charges, internet egress and operational work. No prices are supplied in the exercise because they change by Region and product mode.

Reject answers that equate “private subnet” with “no internet route,” treat NAT as inbound firewall permission, or assume endpoints provide general internet access. This paper exercise has no VPC deployment, route test, measured outage or learner result.

For the NAT-instance extension, do not accept the EC2 hourly line as a complete comparison. Include instance type/size and hours, storage, addresses, processed/transfer traffic, cross-AZ effects, monitoring and the labor to patch, scale, replace and fail over. A low-demand training system that accepts owner-operated interruption may justify evaluating the instance, but no universal cheapest choice follows. A customer workload with a tighter recovery promise must count a monitored failover path and surviving capacity, then compare the same promise for the NAT gateway. AWS recommends gateways for better availability/bandwidth and lower administrative effort; existing legacy NAT AMIs are out of support. No price, capacity test or failure behavior is established by the paper case.
