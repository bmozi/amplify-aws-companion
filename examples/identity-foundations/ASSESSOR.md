# Assessor notes: Five AWS boundaries

Read after the learner's first attempt. This guide names sound reasoning, not a single architecture that every company must choose. The exercise is original and design-only; its cases are not calibrated certification questions. Ask the learner to explain a tradeoff and to identify what evidence could disprove their answer.

1. **Accounts:** A sandbox/prod split can clarify ownership and limit accidental changes, but adds cross-account access, billing, logging, recovery and deployment work. An account boundary is not a guarantee against an authorized shared role or resource-policy path. Trace the exact role and target; do not assume that an SCP grants access or blocks every principal.
2. **Federation:** Workforce identity proves a human identity through a trusted provider; AWS role permissions decide the allowed work. A team move changes assignment, while departure requires stopping new access and considering credentials or sessions already issued. IAM Identity Center and direct federation fit different identity/account arrangements. Customer application sign-in is a separate boundary from AWS workforce access.
3. **Locations:** Availability Zones address some failures within a Region; a second Region can address a broader location failure when the workload, data and dependencies are prepared. Review service/feature availability, data replication and authority, identity, encryption keys, DNS/routing, quotas, external integrations, latency and recurring cost. Do not label “multi-AZ” protection against a Region loss.
4. **Least privilege:** First name caller, required API actions, exact resources, request conditions and lifecycle. Inspect the whole deployment chain, including `iam:PassRole` and any service execution role. A broken first attempt is a reason to diagnose the missing action and evidence, not to grant account-wide authority. Allowing a publisher to change its own trust boundary is a separate escalation risk requiring a changed design.
5. **Shared responsibility:** The division varies by service and feature. AWS operates underlying service components; the customer remains responsible for relevant data, access, configuration and application behavior. The exact split must be checked for the service chosen. Moving to a self-managed VM shifts more operating work to the customer; it does not transfer every AWS facility responsibility.

Award a strong response when it names the boundary, connects a business need to the choice, accounts for the operating/cost consequence, and separates a design claim from evidence actually collected. Do not award “secure,” “highly available,” “least privilege” or “AWS handles it” without the supporting scope and controls. Ask one changed-fact follow-up and require the learner to revise or defend the decision.

The [official SAA-C03 objective](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-certification/latest/solutions-architect-associate-03/solutions-architect-associate-03-domain1.html#solutions-architect-associate-03-domain1-task1) lists the five knowledge areas. The linked manuscript sections provide longer explanations and AWS primary references.
