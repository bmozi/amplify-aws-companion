# Select execution from the job

These original cases are design-only, not AWS exam questions. Read the worked decision in [unit 02](../../reader/BOOK-LOCATORS.md#book-1b1379691a2f6239), answer before opening [assessor notes](ASSESSOR.md), and preserve the assumptions that change your answer.

1. A booking handler usually finishes in 100 milliseconds. Its largest valid request has not been tried. A teammate declares Lambda proven suitable from the average. State a provisional choice and the missing correctness, duration, invocation and dependency evidence.
2. There are 1,000 independent reports, each requiring exactly 30 seconds in a fictional idealized model. All arrive together and must finish within an hour. Compare eight and ten workers. Then account for 1,200 total attempts. Identify excluded delays and why the calculation cannot authorize live concurrency.
3. A job requires 25 minutes of uninterrupted processing and has no safe checkpoint. A proposal uses one standard Lambda event-function invocation. Identify the contract conflict, distinguish other execution modes, and compare redesign with another execution environment. Do not assume that a timeout increase or container image removes the limit.
4. The team selects Fargate and deletes its dependency-update process because AWS patches infrastructure. Identify the mistaken boundary. Change the design to customer-managed EC2 and name the additional operating work.
5. A vendor says its application requires a virtual machine but gives no technical reason. Another engineer says all virtual machines should become containers. Specify the evidence needed to settle the choice and what would reopen it. Include deployment artifacts, host features, supported configuration, data durability and replacement behavior.
6. In the fictional photo pipeline, arrivals are 12/second, workers can process 20/second, and the database saves eight results/second. Each job needs one save. Compare doubling workers with a verified improvement to 16 saves/second. Calculate unfinished work after five minutes at the original rates and its drain time after the database improvement while arrivals continue. Explain why neither a small input queue nor low worker CPU proves timely completion.
7. Start with the 1,200-job backlog from case 6. During a 60-second changeover, arrivals remain 12/second and completions remain eight/second. Then completion capacity becomes 16/second. Calculate the backlog at changeover end and total time from changeover start until it clears. Explain how removing a worker with an unsaved result can violate the model, and specify recovery evidence before reducing capacity.

8. A separate EC2 queue-worker design has 600 visible messages and five in-service instances. Each instance handles one job at a time, averaging two seconds per job; acceptable queue wait is 120 seconds. Calculate a candidate backlog-per-instance target and the same-snapshot instance count. Then processing averages four seconds because of a shared dependency. Explain the changed arithmetic, why it is not an instruction to double capacity, and what zero instances or missing metric data mean.

For each answer include a requirement, candidate, rejected alternative, failure/recovery concern, cost drivers and one observation that could reverse the decision. Ask Codex to supply a competing design, then challenge any unsupported service limit or measured-performance claim.
