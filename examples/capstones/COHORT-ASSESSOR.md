# Assessor: the two incident clipboards

Read this only after your [first capstone response and changed-case predictions](OPERATIONS.md#after-your-first-attempt-reclassify-the-customers). It checks population membership, not incident-management skill by itself.

At second 120, **unfinished** is r3, r4 and r6. **Missed promise** is r3, r4 and r5. r5 completed at 110, after its due time of 100, so it leaves the backlog without leaving the missed-promise record. r6 was accepted at 70 and is not due until 130, so it remains unfinished but is not yet a miss. The mature cohort contains r1–r5 and the on-time result is 2/5.

If r3 completes at 121, then at second 125 **unfinished** is r4 and r6. **Missed promise** remains r3, r4 and r5. The first list shrinks because r3 is done; the second does not because completion at 121 cannot change its deadline at 80. At second 130, r6 is due. If still unfinished, it joins the overdue and missed-promise lists at that exact second; the mature cohort is then all six and the on-time result is 2/6.

A retrospective timeline is useful for learning and post-incident reconciliation, but a live operator at second 120 cannot use a completion observed at 145. The local program rejects a missing or duplicated expected ID, yet the expected list itself is supplied by the fixture. A real source inventory, alert path, customer outcome and mitigation decision need their own evidence. No AWS service or customer action is tested here.
