# Food-bank pickup assessor notes

Use this after making your own decision. A strong answer keeps accepted request, reserved stock and ready pickup distinct and names what would disprove its choice. The local lab is a teaching model, not a reference implementation for a food bank.

1. Each request names a depot, and only an operator authorized for that depot should confirm its stock. Partner authority follows the authenticated actor across record, label, cache, export and direct file access. This one-depot fixture checks only the loopback record/label routes and the operator action; eight-depot isolation remains design work.
2. A request accepted at minute 10 is due at minute 100. If stock is confirmed at minute 100, a purported ready time of minute 20 is earlier than the authoritative stock decision and is rejected. Ready at 101 is `ready` but **late**. The first valid ready time is immutable here, so a replay claiming minute 20 still reports late. The local service owns its fixture clock; real clock sources, delayed events and event ordering need review.
3. A timeout from an external stock system does not prove failure. The [two-ledger fixture](external_stock.py) shows a committed stock reservation and a lost reply leaving pickup state `stock_unknown`. The [HTTP continuation](stock_http.py) moves the stock ledger into a separate local process: after a committed decision and dropped HTTP response, a provider restart and same-ID lookup recover `reserved`. A lookup while it is down remains unknown. A second test stops the provider before any reservation; a later 404 also remains unknown, rather than becoming a false `unavailable`. A second request cannot use a box actually reserved by the first. Stable-ID replay is safe only because the toy provider saves and enforces that contract. With a real provider, verify the key's lifetime, request binding and result lookup. Without an authoritative answer, assign an operator to reconcile the ambiguous effect; do not invent a new reservation ID. Neither local SQLite file gives the two systems a shared transaction; two local processes do not prove cross-host or AWS behavior.
4. Average daily volume cannot bound the busiest minute. Ask for peak arrival distribution, stock replenishment, depot handling time, workers, dependency quotas, acceptable delay, retention, transfer and log volume. A local two-request race is a correctness check, not a capacity test.
5. A real file path requires authorization at the delivery boundary. A private object plus short-lived, actor-authorized issuance is one candidate, but expiry does not instantly revoke a link already issued. Compare proxying through a protected route with direct links under the actual revocation need. This lab returns synthetic bytes, not a stored object or signed URL.
6. Recovery must include request/stock agreement and already issued labels or notifications. Restoring old rows without reconciling external stock or messages can promise a box twice. Compare selective reconstruction with depot-specific recovery boundaries; test both before adopting either.
7. The local provider must reject a missing, wrong or previous-process token before reserve, decision lookup or availability read. The negative test checks these three routes, unchanged stock and an unreadable saved decision; the authorized new client can still read the durable answer. One broad fixture token does not identify a real depot, restrict operations by role or protect plain HTTP. A real service must prove the caller's service identity and depot scope at every route, protect the channel, rotate credentials without losing legitimate in-flight work, and audit denials and stock decisions. A correct answer does not call this fixture production authentication.

The receipt-first candidate is reasonable only when a pending request is acceptable to the business and the customer can see a truthful result. The reserve-first candidate may fit a guaranteed-at-submit promise, provided the synchronous stock dependency, retry ambiguity and deadline are supported. A good cost answer includes the whole path and cleanup, without inventing an AWS price from this fixture. For any later authorized cloud trial, the student should choose an appropriate estimated-run ceiling and credit reserve for the account. No local result here proves a 90-minute service level, real service identity, AWS behavior, or learner performance.
