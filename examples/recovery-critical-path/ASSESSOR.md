# Recovery critical path: assessor reasoning

Read this after writing your own dependency graph. These are answers for stipulated fictional inputs, not a learner score or AWS result.

1. The data chain ends at minute **20**: decision 0–4, key 4–6, restore 6–14, reconciliation 14–20. The worker chain ends at minute **18**: decision 0–4, preparation 4–16, capacity 16–18. Fencing ends at minute **7**. The customer check can start only at minute 20, ends at 23, and route confirmation ends at **25**. The 20-minute target is missed by five minutes in this model.
2. Warm worker preparation ends at minute 6 and capacity proof at 8, but the data branch still ends at 20. The customer and route steps therefore still end at **25**. Paying for ready workers alone does not shorten this modeled recovery. A different dependency or measured duration could change the result.
3. Faster restore alone ends reconciliation at 15, while slow workers and capacity end at 18; customer and route finish at **23**. Combining faster restore with warm workers lets reconciliation finish at 15, capacity at 8 and fencing at 7; the customer check ends at 18 and route confirmation at **20**. Exact fit has no modeled margin for variance.
4. A completed backup copy and a 20-minute graph do not establish a usable recovery point if the destination recovery role cannot decrypt it. B111 missing from both candidate and independently supplied journal leaves a confirmed booking unaccounted for. B110 present at 1,300 rather than 1,200 cents is also a wrong customer result. If the candidate and journal disagree about B111, do not let a convenient source silently win; establish authority and compare the original confirmation. Each condition is `NO_GO` even when the projected clock is green. A new verified recovery point, a corrected tested key/role path or trustworthy independent records might change the answer; assertion alone cannot.
5. Capacity 79 against stipulated demand 80, an unfenced old writer, or a failed customer read/write check each blocks reopening independently. A route switch cannot repair these facts. The local program's booleans are supplied claims, not proof of cloud controls.
6. Removing the reconciliation prerequisite from the customer check makes the scheduler print minute **13** for warm workers. That is a defect in the **plan**, not a recovery improvement. The scheduler trusts the supplied graph; it cannot infer a business dependency the designer omitted. Review the graph against confirmed-booking and single-writer invariants before using its arithmetic.

For transfer, let a new confirmed booking arrive after the journal's observed cutoff. The previously green ID-and-value comparison no longer establishes completeness; obtain an authoritative post-cutoff receipt or keep the decision blocked. A real RPO result needs a declared confirmation boundary and independently verified recovered state. These local minutes cannot be reported as a customer RTO.

The same issue appears when two empty lists match: if the confirmation inventory is not known to be complete, the model holds the plan. Its completeness input is a supplied boolean, not a test of the ledger. New evidence records default to unconfirmed; the worked timing cases explicitly set their fictional checks to true. In a real investigation, identify the independent source, time boundary, responsible owner and reconciliation needed to support that claim.
