# Assessor reasoning for the operational-improvement packet

Use this after an attempt. These are example decisions, not a unique answer key, deployed AWS evidence or a measured learner result. Give credit for a different design when it preserves the customer promise, limits blast radius and names observable proof.

## Initial plan

1. **Measure:** The mature-cohort timely-assignment fraction is 88/100, below 95/100. Add eligible accepted IDs, assignment completion time, queue-entry age, retry outcome, database connection wait and a release/configuration marker to a bounded investigation view. Join logs and sampled traces by correlation ID where present; the eight retries without one are a coverage defect. Protect customer identifiers and choose finite retention. HTTP 200 only proves the probe path responded; 38% average CPU cannot rule out connection saturation, an uneven worker or a slow lookup. The single trace suggests a hypothesis, not population-level cause.
2. **Alert:** Evaluate eligible queue age and mature customer outcomes over a stated window, choose missing-data behavior explicitly, and keep an alert separate from a dangerous automatic repair. Send a test through the selected alarm/action path and retain target acceptance plus operator acknowledgment. If the target fails, retry or escalate through a separately verified route. Neither `ALARM` nor a target API response proves a person acted or customers recovered. The actual AWS alarm and destination semantics need configuration-specific verification.
3. **Deploy:** R7 is not proven causal because queue age rose before 14:00. A small rolling or blue/green wave permits comparison and a stop before whole-fleet exposure; blue/green costs more temporary capacity and needs compatible database/schema and external-effect behavior. All-at-once minimizes overlap but enlarges the initial blast radius. Bind artifact, settings and target to the observation, define a cohort threshold, and keep a rollback path. Reverting traffic cannot recall messages or reverse committed assignments; reconcile those separately.
4. **Automate:** Prefer read-only collection and a bounded drain/restart of one verified bad worker after examining dependency pressure. A restart-all rule could increase simultaneous database reconnections, interrupt in-flight jobs and erase evidence. Limit targets, retries and duration; make job handling idempotent; stop when the customer metric worsens or dependency pressure rises. An operator owns escalation. Automation priority follows reversibility, evidence quality and blast radius, not the ease of writing a rule.
5. **Configuration:** The application concurrency setting should have one versioned release owner and an effective-value check across workers. State Manager is a candidate for desired state on specifically enrolled managed nodes, such as a host-level agent/configuration requirement; it is not proof those nodes are managed or that an application feature rollout happened. AWS Config evaluates recorded resource configuration against rules; it is not a substitute for applying the file to a process. Find targets, documents, permissions and other writers before enabling an association. Resolve conflicts instead of allowing two controllers to fight.
6. **Recovery:** Rehearse into an isolated destination with explicit start/stop clocks. Verify decryptable restore, the last confirmed booking, fencing of the old writer, sufficient worker/database capacity, routing and a successful customer-level booking/assignment path. Record actual RTO/RPO from the declared clocks and durable point; do not assert the objective was met from a backup's existence. Stop if the rehearsal reaches a real customer route or unsafe writer state; inventory and remove temporary resources. This exercise does not run the AWS rehearsal.

## Changed-fact checks

| Change | Reasoned revision | Claim that must remain separate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One slow connection trace, 200 fast acquisitions | Database connection wait is weaker as a general cause; technician lookup becomes a stronger next investigation target. | The 88/100 customer miss remains until a new mature cohort is measured. |
| Concurrency 24 predates release | R7 setting change is not a supported new trigger; verify effective history and look for other changed inputs. | Temporal coincidence alone still cannot prove or disprove all release effects. |
| Target rejects, then accepts retry | One accepted notification attempt can be recorded after a failed attempt. | Human acknowledgment, escalation and customer recovery are still unproved. |
| R6 rollback, duplicate messages | Queue age may improve while external effects remain wrong; reconcile each affected repair and technician message, prevent repeated sends and communicate the correction. | A successful traffic or binary rollback does not undo external effects. |
| Restore misses three confirmations | The stated confirmed-booking recovery promise and RPO are not met; stop promotion and recover/reconcile durable events. | A reachable standby is not a correct recovered business system. |
| Association covers one test node | Inventory the three production workers, local overrides and effective values; resolve ownership and test scope before extending automation. | One test-node compliance result says nothing about three unmanaged production workers. |

## What earns a strong answer

A strong response distinguishes customer outcome from component health; separates an observation from a cause; chooses a measured deployment wave; limits and tests automation; assigns one owner to each setting; rehearses recovery through the customer route; and names cost, authority and cleanup before live work. A generic list of CloudWatch, Systems Manager, CI/CD and backups without these boundaries is incomplete. The packet is a teaching and assessment artifact only. Independent learner attempts, AWS behavior, cost and recovery measurements are still absent.
