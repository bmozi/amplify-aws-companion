# Assessor: repair-status WebSocket design

1. **Wrong repair:** A valid connection is insufficient to hear another customer's job. API Gateway's `$connect` authorization occurs at connection time, so the application needs a job/channel authorization design for later messages and fan-out. AppSync Events has per-operation publish/subscribe authorization, but the chosen channel policy must represent current repair ownership. [API Gateway connection authorization](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/apigateway/latest/developerguide/apigateway-websocket-api-route-keys-connect-disconnect.html), [AppSync Events authorization](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/appsync/latest/eventapi/configure-event-api-auth.html).
2. **Revocation:** The grant change must prevent *future sensitive delivery*. A long-lived connection or token may outlive the business grant. The design should close/unsubscribe affected connections or recheck current authority at delivery under a tested service-specific path. The local model removes Alice's fixture subscription and checks grants for every delivery candidate; it does not prove AWS revocation semantics.
3. **Replay:** The socket itself is not the durable status ledger. After authenticating the new connection and checking current job access, read events after the last accepted sequence from authoritative storage; reject a cursor beyond the known history. Deduplicate by stable event ID/sequence. The first status shown after reconnect must be reconciled with the current business record.
4. **Publisher:** A client may be allowed to send a request or acknowledgment, but it should not set the shop's repair status merely by publishing to a live channel. The trusted worker updates the business ledger under its own authority, then the delivery path fans out that accepted fact. Separate a message receipt from business acceptance.
5. **Scale:** Price connection duration, messages/fan-out, compute, retained ledger/replay reads, logs and transfer for the selected product and Region. A stale-screen indicator should compare the last authoritative status/sequence and delivery age; an open socket is only a transport observation. Duplicate publishing may create duplicate delivery candidates even when the ledger suppresses a second business event.
6. **Device route:** Equipment telemetry has different credentials, topics, validation and replay rules. AWS IoT Core/MQTT may be a better fit than reusing customer WebSocket authorization. The book's [fleet ingestion lesson](../../reader/BOOK-LOCATORS.md#book-f0f0044d800d44b9) keeps the device claim and maintenance fact separate.

**Evidence boundary:** The five local tests cover fixture ownership, revocation, replay, duplicate event identity, and publisher/cursor rejection. They do not open a socket, configure AWS authorization, test delivery after service failure, measure latency or prove independent learning. Treat the design as partial skill evidence, not a functioning customer feed.
