# Assessor: what does pending mean after a lost reply?

Answer from the two-store timeline before reading this page. These are fictional policy and provider capabilities, not universal AWS or email guarantees.

1. **Provider commits, reply disappears.** The outbox still says `pending`; the provider file has one row. Local pending describes an unresolved observation, not proof that nothing happened. `reconcile` joins the stable key and tenant to a provider receipt before marking the local row confirmed.
2. **Lookup returns no receipt.** The local row remains pending. The fixture does not infer success. In a real asynchronous provider, absence might mean the send has not arrived, has failed, or is still processing; a production decision needs that provider's documented timing and query semantics.
3. **Alice loses membership after Shop A accepts the workflow.** This example lets the accepted business work continue. Alice cannot submit new work afterward. That rule comes from the scenario's business policy, not the fact that an outbox exists.
4. **Shop A closes before dispatch.** The fixture stops the pending row and records no provider effect. If closure arrives after the worker's final local check but before a real provider commit, these separate systems have no atomic stop guarantee. A production design must define the cancellation boundary and how an in-flight result is reconciled.
5. **Shop A closes after provider commit.** The receipt still exists and reconciliation records what happened. Closure cannot unsend a message. The business needs a customer/retention response for completed effects.
6. **Provider ignores duplicate keys or cannot return receipts.** The fixture's one-effect claim no longer transfers. A retry may duplicate the action, and a lost reply may remain unknown. Choose a provider-specific idempotency contract or a manual/compensating process before promising one effect.
7. **A receipt has the expected key but another tenant.** The local reconciliation rejects it. A globally unique key is useful, but tenant binding still matters when one service handles several businesses.
8. **Closure falls between the worker's active check and provider commit.** The deterministic test closes Shop A in that window, yet the provider records one effect. The first closure report can truthfully say access is closed and a local effect is unresolved. After a lost reply, a matching receipt changes the effect report to completed-and-needing-retention-review; it never proves deletion of provider copies. Moving the closure before the check changes the local result to stopped. The distinction is the ordering of the last authoritative observation and the separate commit, not the word “closed” on a screen.

The nine external-effect tests cover selected orderings, reopening the two files, a same-key retry, a forged tenant receipt and the injected closure window. They do not simulate worker crashes at every instruction, distributed concurrency, real provider latency, authenticated identities, exactly-once delivery, AWS permissions, account cost or a learner's independent result.
