# Assessor: observability cost is a customer-path choice

The baseline modeled cost is USD 7.890. Its declared critical records, 30-day retention, Standard metric-filter source and operator receipt cover the fictional five-day incident. That is a paper design pass, not evidence of live collection. The USD 3.685 plan is cheaper but cannot answer the complaint: its critical records expire after three days, two required records are absent, the metric-filter source is in Infrequent Access and no operator receipt is declared. Moving to a cheaper class also cannot reclassify an existing log group in place; a new group and revised routing are needed. The USD 4.650 bounded plan retains the four critical records in Standard for seven days, keeps the metric-filter path and a declared operator receipt, and sends verbose data to a separate Infrequent Access group. Its difference from the baseline is a modeled saving of USD 3.240, computed from invented rates, not an account result.

Removing the heartbeat makes silence ambiguous. Retention equal to the incident age is held because the fictional policy requires investigation time beyond the boundary; it is not a claim about precise AWS deletion timing. Changing only the metric-filter source class invalidates the alert path even if the incident records remain. Raising the bounded plan's query scan from 6 to 30 GB changes its modeled total to USD 4.770 but cannot repair a missing record. A reviewer should ask whether the actual system emits the four records with a common incident identity, whether a failed read is visible independently of an accepted receipt, whether data arrives within a useful delay, whether the query and permissions work, and whether a notification reaches a person who can act. A `live_path_verified: false` result remains false for every fixture.

For the zero-usage challenge, hold the design in either case. This fixture assumes both Standard ingestion of critical records and custom metric usage during the modeled period; setting either usage estimate to zero conflicts with that declared path. A zero final charge can still be plausible when an allowance or credit covers usage, but that is a billing question outside these invented usage inputs. Conversely, a positive estimate is only a prediction and cannot establish that the alert was delivered.

The zero stored-GB-month variation also holds: the fictional plan claims to retain incident records during the modeled period but assigns no storage-time to them. One GB held for half a month gives approximately 0.5 GB-month in the teaching model. A tiny nonzero value passes this particular consistency check, but it does not prove complete or accurate volume, retention, billing treatment, or AWS behavior. Credits or allowances may affect the charge without making actual usage zero.

For the repeated-query sensitivity case, the added 800 GB at the invented USD 0.005 per GB adds USD 4.00 to the bounded plan, increasing its modeled total from USD 4.650 to USD 8.650. That exceeds the unchanged baseline's USD 7.890 by USD 0.760. Both declared paths still pass this fixture's checks; no real data, notification or cost was verified. The comparison assumes the stated scan volumes and otherwise unchanged inputs. A fair decision must estimate each plan's actual query frequency and bytes, other meters, operational needs, and whether the retained evidence still meets the incident promise. Higher scan volume alone does not prove the baseline will cost less in a real account.

Accept other designs that preserve the business question and name their actual meters, permissions, privacy and retention duties. Reconcile the diagnostic copy with the authoritative booking record; a log match alone cannot prove that the customer can read the correct appointment. The model omits service-specific charges, actual log volume and compression/retention behavior, alarm evaluation and delivery, human acknowledgment, current rates, credits and a measured customer result. It is not a calibrated exam question.
