# Assessor notes: outside workload identity

1. **First upload:** A trusted CA certificate alone must not imply unrestricted storage access. Check certificate chain and proof of private-key possession, trust anchor, profile, role trust and permission, then all applicable bucket, KMS and organization controls. The allow test writes the intended prefix with the intended encryption context; the deny test attempts a neighboring prefix. An issued session can still lack access to the object or key. [AWS trust model](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/rolesanywhere/latest/userguide/trust-model.html).
2. **Broad CA:** A trust policy that accepts any certificate under a broad CA may admit another valid holder. Bind the role to the intended trust anchor and appropriate certificate attributes, and control which authority can issue those attributes. The exact condition keys and semantics require current documentation and policy review; do not copy a decorative policy fragment. Certificate lifecycle ownership matters as much as the role document. [AWS getting started](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/rolesanywhere/latest/userguide/getting-started.html).
3. **Stolen scanner:** Assume the copied private key can prove possession while the certificate remains acceptable. Disable or constrain the credential issuance path, revoke or replace the certificate where supported and configured, investigate existing sessions and object writes, and rotate the device identity. Revocation-list use is optional in Roles Anywhere; a plan must verify its configuration and timing. Stopping new issuance and ending existing sessions are separate claims. [AWS trust model](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/rolesanywhere/latest/userguide/trust-model.html).
4. **Broken network:** Queue an encrypted, integrity-checked report locally within an explicit retention limit; retry with backoff and an idempotent object/version rule after connectivity returns. Alert on queue age and space. The business must accept or reject the delayed-inventory promise. A permanent key creates another secret to protect and rotate and does not repair the missing network path.
5. **New cloud:** Compare actual supported federation and certificate routes. Verify token issuer, audience, subject, exchange, role trust and short-lived credential scope for federation; verify CA, certificate custody and Roles Anywhere controls for the certificate route. Neither route automatically makes the other cloud's network or data path private. [AWS non-AWS workload guidance](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/IAM/latest/UserGuide/id_roles_common-scenarios_non-aws.html).
6. **New Region/account:** The target account's resource and KMS permissions, workload role path, endpoint and route, event coverage, Region availability, residency rule and transfer cost all need fresh review. A working credential in one account/Region does not prove the destination accepts a write.

**Assessment:** Ask the learner to change one fact after the first answer, such as moving certificate issuance to a shared CA or requiring cross-account encryption. A strong answer revises the actual trust and permission path and names a negative test. A diagram with a single “secure identity” arrow, an access key, or a claim that a certificate itself grants S3 access has missed the central boundary. These notes have not tested AWS configuration, timing, cost or learner performance.
