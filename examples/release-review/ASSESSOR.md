# Assessor notes: integrated release review

Use these criteria to assess the fictional evidence. Do not treat the word “rollback” alone as proof of recovery.

## Packet 1

The qualifying fixture should return 900 cents; the observed B/C2 result contradicts the promise. Startup and health are narrower successes. Hold further exposure and investigate a bounded recovery path. The packet does not distinguish a code defect from configuration or their interaction. It does not establish the nightly caller, old-version compatibility, affected population or safe overlap cost. Returning one route can change later requests on that route; it does not prove every caller switched or reverse an invoice. Immediate deletion may destroy needed recovery or investigation material. Require a reasoned retention decision, not indefinite retention by default.

## Packet 2

A/C2 reproducing the wrong price weakens a B-only explanation. The zero setting supplies a concrete configuration mismatch with the promised discount; schema acceptance did not test that promise. Restoring a supported setting is a candidate remedy that still needs actual consumer/adoption and price checks. The direct B caller is outside the frontend change. The old writer creates a separate data-consistency problem: a successful copy did not stop later legacy writes. Do not assume restoring a configuration fixes that writer.

A strong response separates: bounded exposure control; caller inventory; supported code/configuration recovery; writer coordination and data reconciliation; and business correction of issued invoices. It tests qualifying and nonqualifying prices, preserved unexpired quotes, actual responding identities and intended authorization boundaries. It identifies configuration-writer, deployment/routing and data-correction authority separately. Exact tool commands and resource names cannot be derived from this packet.

I17 is known affected. The full population and payment status are unknown. Find that evidence and the authorized correction policy before asserting a refund or rewriting records. Preserve traceability between the original and any correction. “All invoices repaired” is unsupported.

## Packet 3 and transfer

Closure requires observations, not just the recovery plan: each relevant caller reaches the intended compatible behavior; current readers/writers preserve the data contract; affected records are accounted for; new work behaves correctly; residual resources have owners and lifecycle decisions. Credit/budget facts must be refreshed for an actual run. Accept different stop/escalation conditions when tied to evidence, such as unbounded affected scope, conflicting authoritative records or a failed recovery acceptance probe.

For the access-rule change, treat disclosed data as a consequence that a route switch cannot retract. Bound further access, preserve appropriately restricted evidence, trace affected caller/customer boundaries and involve the responsible security/communication process. Do not expose the private quote again in a teaching report or claim compliance from this exercise. The learner must explain how the consequence changes priorities while reusing the same evidence discipline.

Review the first attempt, the evidence-driven revision and the changed-case answer separately. Passing this design review cannot establish service-specific permissions, propagation, concurrency, alarm delivery, cost or live recovery behavior.
