# Assessor: an allocation is a rule, not a new bill

1. **Measure:** Name a single closed period, currency and cost basis. Include or exclude credits, taxes, refunds, discounts and commitments consistently; document the chosen treatment. The fixture is positive pre-credit cost only. Do not compare its owner totals with a net payment figure or a different period.
2. **Network:** Identify source/destination, direction, metered bytes, fixed-hour components, service and owner. If shared costs have different drivers, split the pool into components rather than impose one ratio blindly. The `3:1` fixture is a declared assumption until those records exist.
3. **AI:** Prefer a billing-native attribution boundary when suitable and activated. Per-request token logs can be a driver, but raw input and output counts across models or modalities are not dollar-equivalent. Confirm logging coverage and price, compute cost weights under a documented versioned rate card, and reconcile the sum to billed dollars. A request-metadata value supplied by the caller needs integrity controls before being accepted as a cost owner.
4. **Dispute:** Keep the original driver file, the new evidence, approver and effective date. The revised allocation moves 750 cents from Repair to Platform and leaves the 16,000-cent source bill intact. A showback statement can display both versions; an internal chargeback needs the business's accepted dispute rule.
5. **Unallocated:** The 1,000 cents cannot be silently distributed merely to force every charge onto a team. Keep a named holding owner, investigation deadline and later correction receipt. A zero unallocated amount is desirable only when evidence supports every assignment.

The script deliberately rejects malformed positive-cost fixtures and uses a consistent largest-remainder rule. It does not parse AWS CUR 2.0, determine network beneficiaries, price tokens, handle real billing adjustments, implement Cost Categories, or prove that the finance organization accepts the allocation. The split-charge view in AWS Cost Categories is distinct from the separately configured CUR split cost allocation data.
