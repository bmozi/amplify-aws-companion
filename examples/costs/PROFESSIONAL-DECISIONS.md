# Professional cost decisions: explain, protect, then optimize

This fictional design packet accompanies [the cost lesson](../../reader/BOOK-LOCATORS.md#book-4ca862dd2539e3b4) and [E11](../../curriculum/EXERCISES.md#e11). It makes no AWS calls, purchases, account changes or price claims. Record an answer for each case before reading the [assessor reasoning](PROFESSIONAL-DECISIONS-ASSESSOR.md). For every recommendation, state the business outcome to preserve, reporting scope and time period, cost measure, assumptions, remaining unknowns, implementation gate and how you would disprove your choice.

1. **The missing $35.** A fictional monthly view shows repair-tagged charges of $40, training-tagged charges of $25, shared identity/network charges of $20 and untagged charges of $15. A teammate reports “repair used 40% of all AWS services.” Another assigns the entire shared $20 to repair. Reconcile the report before credits. Propose an explicit shared-cost policy and state what evidence would support it. What happens if the cost-allocation tag was activated partway through the month?
2. **The quiet tagged dashboard.** A project view reports $0 for yesterday's run; an account/service view shows a new network charge and a retained snapshot. The project tag is present on the app worker only, and the billing observation predates the cleanup. State three distinct explanations or uncertainties and the smallest read-only checks. Would either view establish that the resources are gone now?
3. **The smaller recommendation.** Compute Optimizer suggests a smaller worker from a 14-day utilization lookback. A monthly close runs only on day 28 and must finish by 06:00; its memory peak and recovery rerun were not in the observation period. Decide whether to accept, reject or hold the suggestion. Define a safe test and rollback. Then change the case: the worker really is idle every night, but the retained database and backup remain necessary. Which costs can the change address?
4. **The storage cleanup.** Storage Lens highlights old incomplete uploads and many noncurrent object versions. Some versions are the only approved historical recovery points after a logical error. Distinguish the two candidates and define retention-owner, restore and cost checks before a lifecycle change. State whether a paid advanced metric tier is justified for this one question.
5. **The attractive commitment.** A team has two years of highly variable compute use. It wants to buy a three-year Savings Plan solely because a monthly recommendation shows a discount. Compare short On-Demand use, a measured stable baseline with a suitably scoped commitment, and an overcommitted plan. Explain why applying a discount to a wrong-sized fleet is not the first optimization. Do not invent actual AWS rates or make a purchase.
6. **The cheaper recovery fleet.** A nightly report can be retried from a checkpoint before its morning deadline; a customer-facing booking path must stay available during an AZ failure. Compare Spot and dependable capacity for each path. State the interruption, placement, quota and deadline evidence needed. Explain separately whether a regional Reserved Instance would reserve recovery capacity.
7. **The cheap photo archive.** Maya must keep repair photos for an approved period and retrieve a disputed photo within one hour. A teammate picks the storage class with the lowest listed storage price and proposes a second-Region copy. Draw upload, retained copies, retrieval and customer delivery paths. Compare this candidate with a faster-retrieval class under the same retention and recovery promise. List the request, storage, minimum-duration, retrieval, transfer, fixed-service and logging meters that may apply; distinguish a meter to verify from a charge the design definitely incurs. State which current Region-specific prices and workload quantities are missing. Would a budget warning, promotional credit, or compute Savings Plan make the one-hour restore promise or the total estimate valid? Change the case so only one percent of photos are ever reopened, then change the restore deadline to one day. Explain which candidate might become feasible without inventing prices.

Ask Codex to challenge one answer by changing the business deadline, removing the shared-cost allocation evidence, or adding an untagged service. Require it to distinguish a recommendation, an estimate, a measured bill and a verified customer outcome. Use a script for any new arithmetic. No learner trial or live optimization is claimed.
