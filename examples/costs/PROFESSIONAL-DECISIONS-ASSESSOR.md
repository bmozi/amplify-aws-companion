# Professional cost decisions: assessor reasoning

These notes judge the supplied fictional cases. They are not an observed AWS bill, purchasing recommendation, learner score or deployment procedure.

1. The pre-credit total is $100, with $65 directly tagged and $35 shared or untagged. A tag does not by itself assign the shared $20. A defensible allocation rule could use a measured share of identity requests or a clearly disclosed fixed split agreed with finance; each has different incentives. Keep unsupported $15 unallocated until its owner is established. Confirm tag activation, coverage/backfill and the report period before assigning earlier charges. Reconcile the departmental and explicit unallocated rows to the same account-wide total and measure. Never treat an account credit as a resource tag.
2. The narrow view could exclude an untagged network charge, show data before billing refresh, or omit the retained snapshot. Inspect filter, measure, usage period, observation time, tag activation and broader service/account rows, then compare with exact resource inventory and removal evidence. Billing views report usage; they are not live inventories or hard spending limits.
3. Hold the resize until the monthly close, memory peak and recovery rerun are represented. Test the candidate under the meaningful workload and failure window, watch customer completion and keep a reversible prior configuration. If the worker is genuinely idle at night, scheduled stop/scale-down can address eligible running-capacity hours; database, backup, storage and other residual charges remain until separately changed under their own requirements. A low average CPU graph does not override the deadline.
4. Old incomplete multipart uploads may be waste if no needed operation depends on them; noncurrent versions may be recovery assets. Inventory the exact objects/versions, retention obligations and restore use, then price storage and transitions/requests for the selected Region. Test an isolated restore before deleting historical points. Start with the available free metric scope and justify any paid advanced tier from the additional question it answers and its cost.
5. Savings Plans commit hourly spend for a term; a modeled discount on eligible usage is not proof of overall savings if the fleet shrinks, is idle or changes eligibility. Establish the stable eligible baseline from representative periods, reduce unnecessary usage and size first, then compare a conservative commitment and uncovered variable usage with On-Demand. Check utilization, coverage and full-term cash obligation. A short personal lab has no evidence justifying a multi-year purchase.
6. A checkpointed report may use Spot if it can restart and complete before the deadline after interruption or unavailable capacity. Test the adverse timing and allowed alternatives. Keep dependable capacity for the booking baseline and AZ-survival requirement, with placement, database and quota evidence. A regional RI provides a matching discount but does not reserve AZ capacity; a zonal RI has different matching and reservation behavior. Neither discount alone proves a booking can be served.
7. Reject a choice based only on stored-byte price. Begin with approved retention, read frequency, object size, one-hour retrieval and the actual restore workflow. A class whose restore process cannot meet that deadline is ineligible even if its stored-byte line is smallest. For each remaining class, estimate retained bytes and copies, transitions, requests, retrieval and applicable minimum-duration charges with dated class/Region terms. Draw each actual transfer boundary and price its direction and volume separately, including customer delivery; a second-Region copy adds a recovery path and potential copy/transfer/storage charges but does not automatically make retrieval timely. Check whether an endpoint, managed service, logs or encryption-related requests introduce fixed or per-operation meters. Mark unused components as absent rather than adding every possible AWS charge. Compare low, expected and adverse read volumes under the same promise, and keep an unknown-price line visible. A smaller read fraction changes the cost mix; a one-day deadline may admit slower retrieval after a restore test, but neither change alone proves the cheapest class. A budget alert can arrive after usage, credits change payment, and a compute commitment only discounts eligible usage. None validates recovery or caps the whole bill. No purchase or live restore is warranted by this fictional case.

Reward explicit customer, accounting and time boundaries. Reject a service-name answer that silently changes the promise. Any real action would require a current account, support/feature eligibility, selected-region prices, scoped authority and a tested rollback. Independent technical and learner review remain open.
