# Pricing-commitment assessor: arithmetic and decision

Run the script before relying on these totals. Under its stated fictional assumptions, six busy hours cost USD 4.8000 On-Demand and USD 4.0000 with the simplified commitment: USD 0.8000 modeled savings. The falling schedule costs USD 2.6000 On-Demand and USD 3.2000 with the commitment: USD 0.6000 more. Two idle hours still each owe USD 0.40 in this model.

Changing the light hour to a busy one increases usage, but the six-hour result still cannot justify a real purchase. The term is much longer, use can move or disappear, and AWS applies actual discounts and eligible usage according to the selected offering. A competent decision record names the workload and business outcome, representative utilization window, eligible baseline after waste removal, full-term cost under low/normal/high demand, exact current terms, owner and revisit trigger. It keeps credits and potential plan changes separate from pre-credit resource cost.

For this cloud-free exercise, the correct action is **no commitment purchase and no plan upgrade**. A favorable fictional row teaches arithmetic; it supplies neither purchasing authority nor evidence that credits would cover a charge.
