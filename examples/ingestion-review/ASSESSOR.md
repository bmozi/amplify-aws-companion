# Ingestion decision assessor notes

Use these explanations after considering [the cases](README.md). Accept different designs when their assumptions and evidence satisfy the actual requirement.

1. A batch is a credible starting candidate because no intraday deadline is stated. Define expected source files/events, late arrivals, validation, reruns and time left for repair before 08:00. Streaming may become useful under a changed deadline or another justified consumer need; an extra continuously operating component is not automatically better.
2. Workers sharing a queue distribute work; this does not promise every independent application its own full history. Investigate fan-out to separate queues or independently progressing stream consumers. Specify duplicates, per-consumer failure handling and replay needs. Do not infer one global order or exactly-once business effects.
3. The progress marker can cause restart to skip an effect that never committed. Saving progress earlier is not the remedy for duplicate processing. Require a durable-effect/progress protocol appropriate to the selected systems and a crash test at this boundary.
4. Repeating an unconditional increment can double-count. A stable event identity plus a correct transactional destination rule can recognize repeated application while distinguishing conflicting content. That local rule does not make an external email/payment atomic. Test effect committed plus progress absent; a happy-path count does not cover it.
5. A contiguous progress marker through 43 would falsely imply 42 was accounted for. Hold progress behind the unresolved gap, or use a supported mechanism and explicit durable error policy that accounts for it. Merely logging an exception is not a repair ledger. This is protocol reasoning, not a claim about a configured client library.
6. The excess rate is 20/second for 300 seconds, so backlog is 6,000. The later spare rate is 20/second, so draining needs 300 seconds. At 100 arrivals and 100 completions per second, the backlog does not drain in this constant-rate model. Real work varies in size and duration; retries, uneven keys and downstream limits need measurement.

For the transfer challenge, require a replay source that covers the intended interval, separate output identity/version, a plan for duplicates and side effects, bounded access and a full cost estimate. Reprocessing a retained dataset is not proof that the source originally captured every real-world event. Record the learner's assumptions and evidence limits beside the design.

For the seventh case, require the byte-to-bit conversion: 30 MB at 10 Mb/s needs 24 seconds in this decimal-unit model. Total delays are 375 seconds at the five-minute interval and 255 seconds at the three-minute interval. Ninety MB needs 72 seconds of transfer, giving 303 seconds total and missing the target. Adding the transfer challenge's 60-second retry to 255 gives 315 seconds, also missing it. Require explicit assumptions about batching, sustained throughput, no overlapping stages, worker availability and complete publication. Accept alternative pipeline designs only when the answer revises the timing model and names the observations needed. These are arithmetic/model checks, not live performance or learner evidence.

## Ingestion-authority cases

1. Permission to write a stream is distinct from permission to assert a tenant's business facts. Require trusted membership/source mapping or an explicitly justified resource boundary. A body field or partition label alone is insufficient. A gateway proposal must address direct producer access around it. Stream receipt is separate from ledger acceptance.
2. The endpoint policy constrains its own path. Inspect applicable IAM/resource policies and test alternate paths before claiming endpoint-only access. The intended endpoint still needs allowed/denied identity/action/resource tests. Avoid prescribing an unreviewed blanket deny that could break required service calls.
3. If IDs are producer-scoped, use trusted producer identity plus event ID consistently in the destination key and expected manifest. Each authorized shop's `42` can be distinct; an unchanged retry within one scope is a duplicate, changed content within that scope is a conflict. Reject unauthorized submissions before treating them as accepted business events. The event lab now has a separate producer-scope pass that implements composite identity and manifest checks; its producer context remains trusted and does not establish this authentication boundary.
4. Discover the actual read/checkpoint/output operations, scope them to required resources and avoid administrative powers without a demonstrated need. Use separate candidate outputs, bounded replay and checks for customer-facing side effects. Reading historical records does not require permission to delete the stream. A complete plan also identifies output promotion authority and recovery.

For the compromised-sender transfer, accept that schema checks cannot prove a sale occurred. Require independent business reconciliation where available and an incident process; do not claim rate limits establish truth. These are provisional design criteria with no live authorization or learner outcome. Sources: [Kinesis actions](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/service-authorization/latest/reference/list_kinesis.html) and [endpoint guidance](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/streams/latest/dev/vpc.html), checked October 18, 2026.
