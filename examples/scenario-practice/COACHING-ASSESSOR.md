# Assessor reasoning: protect the attempt, then challenge it

This is an authored review of the [fictional coaching transcript](COACHING-REVIEW.md), not an observed learner result or an independent assessment.

1. The assistant supplied “B” before the learner chose or explained anything. The learner's later “I guess B” is assisted. The exchange can teach what to inspect next, but it cannot be counted as an unaided correct answer or mastery. Preserve it as an assisted attempt; use a different unseen case for an independent one.
2. Flow A acknowledges before durable capture, so a restart can erase accepted work. It also changes request IDs on browser retry, weakening duplicate recognition. Flow B has the better *proposed ordering*, but the words “durable queue or store” do not prove durability, caller authorization, retained request identity, redelivery behavior, supplier idempotency, safe reconciliation or customer-visible completion. Test acceptance before/after an injected worker restart, duplicate delivery under one ID, a supplier commit with a lost reply, cross-caller ID collision and the final order record. A queue alone does not make the supplier side effect exactly once.
3. A suitable prompt is: “Here is an original scenario and my sealed first answer. Do not give an answer letter yet. Identify one requirement my reasoning may have missed and ask me to revisit it. After my revision, give one changed condition that could reverse the choice. Mark claims needing current primary documentation or a local test. Do not score my mastery.” The reader must still check the cited service behavior against the precise service documentation and run or inspect a bounded implementation where the claim requires it.
4. **Pending** can be a truthful early receipt only if the product clearly distinguishes it from **Accepted**. The later **Accepted** transition still needs durable capture evidence. Without supplier lookup or an idempotent operation contract, a lost reply may leave the team unable to tell whether retrying would duplicate the external order. The design must hold an explicit unknown state, seek an alternative reconciliation channel or change the supplier/product contract; a blind retry does not solve that uncertainty. Flow B remains a useful internal acceptance pattern, not proof of an exactly-once external effect.
5. A valid record labels the first answer as unavailable or contaminated, quotes the assistance actually received, gives the learner's later reasoning as a coached revision, and separates the changed-case explanation. Missing evidence includes real store durability, caller authorization, supplier reconciliation, customer outcome, selected-service cost and cleanup. The scope is fictional design practice; no AWS, supplier, model or learner-transfer test happened.

Credit reasoning that preserves the distinction between *accepted intake* and *completed supplier order*. A reader may propose a different architecture if it meets both stated promises and identifies effective evidence. Do not require a particular AWS service name. The goal is to defend the invariant and its failure path, then adjust when the contract changes.
