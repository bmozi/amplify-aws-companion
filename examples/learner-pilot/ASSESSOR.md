## Interpret the pilot conservatively

Separate source navigation, vocabulary, concept explanation, changed-case reasoning and optional execution. A tool-installation problem is not automatically a conceptual failure. A participant who corrects a misconception after a hint has demonstrated an assisted revision, not unaided mastery. Keep the earlier answer.

For each observed problem, record the exact passage/step, the participant evidence, a proposed remedy and what would test that remedy. A content error warrants correction; an unclear word may need an explanation; an untested stylistic preference remains a preference. Avoid rewriting a whole chapter from one vague reaction.

After changing material, use a new analogous case instead of repeating the memorized answer. Report source versions, participants' relevant experience, assistance and unresolved observations. Two participants can expose defects and suggest improvements; they cannot validate the whole book, establish certification readiness or prove superiority over another resource.
