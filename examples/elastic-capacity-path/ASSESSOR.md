# Assessor reasoning: three capacity dials

Use after an independent first attempt. Accept a defensible alternative when its assumptions, evidence and customer effect are explicit. The numbers below describe a fictional steady snapshot, not AWS throughput guarantees.

1. **Baseline:** 60 × 0.4 = 24 safe hits/s; 36 origin requests/s. Writes are six/s and origin reads 30/s. The two workers supply 40 origin requests/s; database writes allow eight/s; database reads allow 28/s. The read allowance is exceeded by two/s. Observe read wait/throttling or database load together with request latency, rather than inferring the cause from one average CPU chart.
2. **More clerks:** Three workers supply 60 origin requests/s, but origin reads remain 30/s against 28/s. The database read limit still binds. Inspect scaling activity and healthy in-service targets separately from end-to-end latency and correctness.
3. **More safe hits:** Thirty hits/s leave 30 origin requests/s, 24 reads/s and six writes/s. All supplied steady ceilings fit. Test an origin unavailable/cold-cache interval and compare public response freshness; a p99 or burst may still fail.
4. **Private response:** One shared key could leak one customer's booking to another. Keep personalized results out of a shared cache unless a reviewed policy demonstrably partitions by the correct actor and invalidates at the needed time; often the safer design is no shared caching of this response. A hit-rate gain does not justify disclosure or stale booking state.
5. **RDS:** Storage autoscaling reacts to low free storage; it does not make a broad scan efficient. Examine plan/index/query and load first. A read replica can serve eligible reads with an explicit consistency contract but does not enlarge the primary write capacity. Record ongoing instance, storage, transfer and operation cost, and a reversal path; RDS storage autoscaling does not shrink allocated storage.
6. **DynamoDB:** Use the throttling reason to distinguish an index write or key-range limit from a table read limit. Provisioned index write auto scaling has its own range and reaction delay; a hot key may need a data-access redesign even in on-demand mode. Public-read caching does not repair write back-pressure.
7. **Changed mix:** 12 writes/s, 18 origin reads/s and 30 origin requests/s. The database's eight writes/s now binds. Inspect affected write/throttle/latency and correctness signals, then choose a bounded change with a rollback/stop threshold. Reducing read load further cannot increase supplied write allowance.

The local test proves exact arithmetic for the supplied inputs, rejection of an impossible shared-hit claim and recognition of zero ready workers. It does not test a CloudFront cache policy, ElastiCache invalidation, EC2 scaling policy, RDS resize/replica, DynamoDB throughput or customer experience. Independent learner review is pending.
