# One business change, five architect questions

Maya's repair shops use a signed-in booking site. A customer receives a confirmation, but after one Region fails, the standby site does not show that booking. Maya is also buying a third shop whose staff and old records must join the business. Her team proposes a second Region, a new AI assistant for repair-policy questions, and automatic releases. A vendor slide calls the whole proposal “highly available and AI ready.” What should an architect ask before approving any of it?

The [five-question map](five-domain-map.svg) follows the **same customer promise** through the five domains in AWS's [published SAP-C03 exam guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-certification/latest/solutions-architect-professional-03/solutions-architect-professional-03.html). The guide names 21 tasks and 115 numbered skills; our [dated source inventory](../../curriculum/SAP-C03-BASELINE.md) records the count and a bounded teaching route. This original practice case crosses those domains, but a domain connection is not proof that a reader can perform every skill in it. Keep the older SAP-C02 ledger separate through the [transition record](../../curriculum/SAP-C03-TRANSITION.md).

First, write one sentence for the business promise: “A confirmed booking remains findable to authorized staff and customers after the declared failure within the agreed time and data-loss limits.” Decide what counts as *confirmed*, who may read it, how long recovery may take, and whether the acquired shop's staff may see it. Those are owner decisions, not service names.

## Make the architecture decision

Start with an exact objective rather than trying to memorize a domain name. In the published [Domain 4 guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/aws-certification/latest/solutions-architect-professional-03/solutions-architect-professional-03-domain4.html), Task 4.2 concerns disaster recovery. Skill 4.2.1 asks for a recovery strategy based on recovery time, recovery point and cost; Skill 4.2.4 concerns multi-Region failover. Case 1 below practices part of that decision: two sites can each accept a booking, so a fast route switch could still show the wrong appointment. The task tells you what to study. It does not tell you which writer is authoritative in this fictional shop, and solving this case does not complete either skill. Record the guide date, task ID, first answer and missing implementation proof separately.

Answer each changed case **before** opening the [assessor notes](ASSESSOR.md). For each, write: (1) the business promise at risk, (2) the first missing fact, (3) two bounded alternatives, (4) one observation that could reject your preferred choice, (5) a cost/cleanup implication, and (6) which of the five domains the answer crosses. A single service name is not an answer.

1. **Two Regions, two writers.** The standby page loads and its database accepts a new booking. The original Region returns with a different booking in the same appointment slot. Should traffic move back immediately? Name the rule that decides whose booking survives and a customer receipt that would expose the wrong answer.
2. **Acquired staff.** The new shop's staff can sign in to the company directory but have no explicit assignment to the booking account. Someone proposes broad access so the migration can start. What can sign-in prove, and what separate grant and denial must be checked?
3. **AI policy helper.** Retrieval finds a relevant policy document from another shop and an old document that says to issue credits. The assistant proposes a credit tool call. Where do evidence access, policy meaning and action authority each belong? What would cause the system to abstain?
4. **Cheap standby.** A spreadsheet prices one hour of compute in the standby Region and nothing else. The owner asks whether the proposed rehearsal fits the team's run limit and preserves its credit reserve. Identify the missing meters before calculating a decision. Then use the invented complete worksheet below; explain why passing its arithmetic still cannot authorize a live run.
5. **Automatic recovery.** A runbook receives a green application health check and is ready to change DNS. The last confirmed booking is absent from the candidate Region, and the attempt telemetry feed stopped. What should the runbook do? What independent evidence would allow a later decision?
6. **Successful release, wrong booking.** A canary and deployment controller report success. The new release reads a different data version and shows the wrong appointment time. What should the release gate have checked, and what does rollback require if new writes occurred?

**Transfer:** Change the business from repair appointments to a school document-review portal. Keep the five questions, but replace the booking invariant with “an approved document version is available only to its owner after scan and distinct human review.” Which answers survive unchanged, and which must be redesigned? Use the [file-intake review path](../file-intake/REVIEW-PATH.md) if useful. The exercise is original practice, not a calibrated mock exam or learner result.

## Test the standby cost decision twice

Imagine two people checking the last open seat. Each can see it, but both cannot claim it. A credit balance has the same planning problem when two runs use one reserve. This is **fictional arithmetic**, not AWS pricing or a credit read.

For case 4, the team chooses a **USD 9 per-run limit**, starts with **USD 100 of eligible credits**, and wants to leave **USD 90** for later work. The proposed rehearsal tests failover only; it does not deploy the AI assistant or acquired-shop integration. A revised worksheet contains these invented full-run amounts: compute USD 1.50; replica, backup and storage USD 1.75; transfer USD 2.25; DNS, logs and alarms USD 0.75; test traffic, teardown and retained charges USD 0.50. Add a 25% uncertainty allowance to the subtotal. A separate operator also proposes a **USD 3 whole-run estimate including its own allowance**. Neither has recorded an active-run claim yet.

Before using a calculator, answer three questions: (1) Does the first run pass both chosen numbers? (2) Could the team start both runs from the same USD 100 snapshot while preserving the reserve? (3) What fact is missing even if the numbers pass? Keep your prediction. The [editable case file](standby-cost.json) contains exactly the amounts above. From the repository root, run:

```sh
python3 examples/sap-c03-bridge/standby_cost.py
python3 -m unittest discover -s examples/sap-c03-bridge -p 'test_*.py' -v
```

Read the separate answers: each run fits the USD 9 per-run limit, but their combined USD 11.4375 commitment leaves USD 88.5625, below the USD 90 reserve. Now copy `standby-cost.json` outside the repository, change `replica_backup_storage` from `1.75` to `2.75`, and pass the copy's path to `standby_cost.py`. Predict which additional hold appears before running it. The revised subtotal is USD 7.75; with the same 25% allowance the standby run becomes USD 9.6875. It now breaks its own USD 9 limit *and* the shared reserve. A missing meter or negative quote is rejected instead of becoming a misleading green result.

Next run the [local two-operator credit exercise](../costs/credit-commitments/README.md) and explain what its **different fictional amounts** prove about a shared commitment. The standby checker does arithmetic on one file. The SQLite register coordinates only cooperating local processes. Neither reserves AWS credits, caps a bill or authorizes a live run. Current account prices, credit eligibility and balance, selected resources, scoped identity, active-run claim, stop rule and cleanup review remain separate prerequisites.
