# Encrypted recovery: provisional assessor notes

Use after the learner answers the [packets](README.md). These are authored criteria, not results from a learner trial. Reward a justified revision when the evidence changes. Record the learner's actual explanation; do not translate this short exercise into a certification score.

## 1. Inventory before retirement

The earlier export still identifies A. B's successful new reads and seven quiet days do not establish that A is unnecessary. The checksum supports byte identity against the recorded digest; it does not establish decryptability, completeness, correct authorization or the trustworthiness of the original recording process. A useful test identifies the exact retained export, its metadata, the recovery identity, supported reader and expected synthetic records. It tests actual reads and business reconciliation without deleting or disabling a recovery dependency. A single successful export is bounded evidence, not coverage of every retained copy.

## 2. Follow the caller

The supplied comparison points toward the recovery caller's authorization path. It does not prove which policy statement or condition is responsible. Inspect the exact principal/session, key identity and Region, requested operation, key policy, applicable IAM policies/grants and relevant conditions or denials. Fetching the object and using its key are separate permissions. Avoid guessing that an IAM allow alone resolves every key-policy arrangement. The other role's success does not authorize copying its broad permissions.

## 3. Separate preserved, usable and recovered

Cancellation preserved the key in the scenario, but the observed Disabled state is not readiness for KMS cryptographic use. A reviewed recovery procedure must establish usable key state and intended caller access, then prove a fresh supported read and application result. This is a reasoning exercise; no command or real key-state change is needed.

For the specified symmetric key and still-wrapped data key, replacing a deleted key or reusing its alias cannot reconstruct the original decryption capability. Do not generalize this narrowly specified case into claims about every asymmetric, imported-material or multi-Region arrangement. Likewise, a running process can retain a plaintext data key: continued warm-process success is not proof that a fresh recovery reader can unwrap its key. Never retain or expose plaintext keys as an exercise shortcut.

## 4. Reconcile before release

The supplied report supports decryption and the expected export record count. It does not justify reopening the application with records that the deletion ledger says must remain removed. Require reconciliation against the protected ledger and evidence of intended reads and denied access to removed records. Preserve actual hold/retention obligations if the case changes; those require accountable business decisions. Do not invent a recovery-time result: the packets give neither a target nor timing evidence.

## Transfer

Rotation within A retains the logical key and its ability to select appropriate older material. It neither re-encrypts those exports nor replaces their data keys. It does not make A dispensable. A strong answer distinguishes changing key material, replacing the logical key and migrating existing protected packages.

## Evidence boundary

This exercise assesses reasoning from supplied facts only. It performs no cryptographic operation, KMS policy test, backup restore, deletion, timed recovery or customer authorization test. A future implementation would need service-specific integration evidence, approved identity and cost scope, synthetic fixtures, cleanup planning and an actual outcome record. Choose an appropriate estimated-run ceiling and credit reserve before a live implementation; this paper exercise needs no cloud run.
