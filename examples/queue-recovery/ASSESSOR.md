# Queue recovery: assessor reasoning

Authored criteria only; no independent learner trial or AWS execution.

1. Visibility is a transport-delivery control, not a business authorization or exclusive supplier lock. Both attempts can overlap in the stipulated sequence. Preserve J's stable operation identity and reconcile the supplier result. Extending visibility may reduce premature redelivery but does not remove the effect-before-acknowledgment failure window.
2. Receives can occur without a supplier call. Inspect the relevant processing stage, call identity and trusted provider evidence; absence of a log line alone is not proof no effect happened. A timeout after a call leaves a different uncertainty from a demonstrably pre-call crash. Do not replay as a new business operation merely because a receive count increased.
3. Investigate the producer/schema mismatch and the permitted transformation or correction. Preserve original evidence and trace any reviewed replacement to the original business identity. Repeatedly delivering unchanged invalid data does not fix validation. Authorization failure needs the actor/action/resource reviewed against the requirement; an intended denial is not a defect to remove.
4. Standard: nominally four hours remain, since 24 minus 20 equals four. The one hour in the DLQ does not create a fresh 24-hour lifetime. FIFO: under the stipulated timestamp reset, nominally 23 hours remain. These calculations concern retention clocks, not a service deletion-time measurement or a guarantee of remaining recovery time. The customer request's age and deadline remain separate.
5. State that no messages remain in the inspected DLQ, while two accepted requests are still unresolved in the ledger and one supplier effect is unknown. Check replay destination/processing results, deletion/expiry evidence where available, stable operation IDs and supplier receipts. Verify exact business outcomes, including duplicate effects and any compensating action. Empty transport storage does not establish customer completion.

A future live test needs a scoped identity, selected queue type/configuration, synthetic messages, controlled worker failures, observed redelivery/redrive and effect reconciliation, retention handling, monitoring and cleanup evidence. It must fit the account owner's chosen estimated-run limit and preserve an appropriate credit reserve; this assessment authorizes no cloud run. Review the selected Lambda integration separately rather than assuming all consumers manage acknowledgment identically.


## Independent subscriber cases

6. Workers sharing a queue divide a responsibility; that does not establish that two different applications each receive every event. A proposed SNS topic with separate scheduler and audit queue subscriptions gives each a distinct delivery path. The scheduling queue can still have multiple workers. Require appointment and audit records separately, plus configured delivery permissions, duplicate handling, monitoring and recovery. No live fanout or configuration is demonstrated by the drawing.
7. Scheduling is recorded for J2; audit remains unresolved. Investigate publication, subscription eligibility, delivery and consumer state before classifying its absence. Avoid a new appointment effect to repair an audit-only gap. An authorized audit replay preserves J2's identity and verifies the missing audit result; a common replay route also requires the scheduling consumer to recognize prior work. Inspect final business records, not only queue emptiness.
8. The stipulated filter excludes ordinary events from that subscription, conflicting with the requirement. No failed-queue entry does not establish successful audit processing or universal source completeness. Correct the reviewed configuration and recover the bounded missing population from an authoritative retained source, if available. Determine identities, interval, destinations and duplicate-effect protections before replay. If no source can reconstruct the omitted events, report the evidence gap; changing the filter cannot manufacture history.

## Routing case

Case 9 separates content-based routing from competing consumption. EventBridge rules are a candidate for routing matching events to supported integrations; SNS subscription filtering can also satisfy some fan-out requirements. Require the learner to justify the selected target support, schema/filter behavior and operating needs rather than award credit for a service name alone. One shared competing-consumer queue does not independently deliver every event to finance and audit. Audit must not inherit a finance-only filter. Track acceptance, target delivery and completed business effects separately.

For target failure, check the selected service's retry window, permissions and failure destination; an exhausted delivery needs a recoverable record and reviewed replay. A new filter does not manufacture historical delivery. Explain which durable source or configured archive could supply the missing events and how duplicates and external effects will be reconciled. No AWS behavior is executed by this paper case.
