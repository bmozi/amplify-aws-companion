## DOP-C02 task 3.1 resilience design route

Use the learner's stated business promise as the grading key. Multiple designs can be reasonable; unsupported guarantees are not.

1. **Business requirement:** The answer distinguishes the fictional calendar-time availability SLO from correct durable booking completion and from any provider SLA. A 30-day 99.9% availability target corresponds to about 43 minutes 12 seconds of unavailable time, but that arithmetic is a planning aid rather than measured performance or a service guarantee. Require the learner to say whether five minutes of AZ degradation counts as a missed objective and what proof is retained. A zero-loss requirement changes the data and uncertain-write design; it is not met by merely increasing replica count.
2. **Single points of failure:** Expect the learner to test shared dependencies and operator/recovery paths, not only count app instances. One shared identity provider, key, writer, DNS control, deployment artifact or external service can remain a single point. A managed service is not an automatic duplicate of every dependent resource. Repair means name a feasible redundancy or recovery mechanism and the evidence required; labeling something “multi-AZ” is not evidence.
3. **Cross-Region support:** Expect a selected feature and Region compatibility check against primary AWS documentation, plus data freshness, keys, authorization, quotas, writer ownership, promotion/restore, cost and cleanup. The scenario intentionally excludes Organizations and cross-account rollout; the exercise does not authorize organization-dependent configuration. A design may select an alternate service when its constraints do not fit, but must explain the changed semantics.
4. **Cross-AZ load balancing:** The learner should identify cross-zone target placement, health-check scope, surviving capacity and application admission control. If the case uses an ALB and all registered targets are unhealthy, AWS documents fail-open behavior: it can route to all targets rather than treating the target group as a reliable deny gate. The answer must not claim that an unhealthy status by itself protects customers from failed work.
5. **Multi-location application readiness:** Require a single write authority, handling for the unknown outcome of an interrupted write, measured/declared replication delay, stale DNS client strategy, quota-aware degraded service, and separate failback steps. The learner must not claim all traffic is moved instantly or full capacity exists because an endpoint responds. The reopen signal should include correct customer records and completion behavior.

These cases map the objective's skills to a design response only. No SLA was validated, no infrastructure was configured, no cross-Region copy or load balancer was created, and no failover, downtime, data loss, billing or learner result was observed.

1. **Global infrastructure:** An AZ and a Region are different failure scopes. A multi-AZ layout may address selected AZ failures but does not make a second Region ready. Resources are not universally copied automatically. Require a service-by-service inventory of compute, configuration, data, identity, keys, routing, quotas and recovery control.
2. **Managed services:** Comprehend analyzes text; Polly synthesizes speech. AWS Managed Services (AMS) provides ongoing infrastructure operations management and can make specific AWS services available through its own offerings. Do not grade the acronym as though it makes Comprehend or Polly the same product. A queued classification can remain pending while an explicitly designed booking path continues. The team still owns business correctness, data classification, permissions, fallback, retry limits and customer messaging.
3. **Network path:** Route-table destinations select a network target. Security groups and other controls decide whether traffic is allowed. A timeout alone does not identify which failed. Ask for route-table association/target, flow and connection evidence, and a deliberately scoped permission test.
4. **Distributed design:** Separate resource pools or queue-backed work can contain a slow optional job, but every new boundary adds state, retries, poison-message handling, status, alarms, access and cost. Require idempotent effects and bounded queue age. Reject an unbounded retry loop that competes with bookings.
5. **Immutable replacement:** Build a new identifiable artifact from corrected source, test it, promote it through a controlled path, and retain a tested rollback or forward repair. A manual change is drift until reflected and reviewed in source. Recreating from the old artifact would reintroduce the finding.
6. **Load balancing:** ALB health checks normally affect target eligibility, but when every target in the target group is unhealthy, it fails open and routes to all targets. A good answer names this behavior and adds application-level admission/overload handling. Health endpoints must reflect a deliberate readiness contract; neither a shallow process check nor an indiscriminate dependency check proves the entire customer operation.
7. **Proxy:** RDS Proxy can pool/reuse connections and help applications handle supported RDS/Aurora failovers. AWS says in-flight statements or transactions can be canceled. The proxy cannot resolve the business outcome of an interrupted write. Require a stable operation key, authoritative read/reconciliation and duplicate-safe retry before making another booking.
8. **Quotas/throttling:** Compare applied quotas, resource limits and required peak recovery capacity by Region and service. An increase request is not a reservation. Throttling needs bounded backoff and customer-safe deferral; optional classification should not multiply retries or consume the booking limit. Estimate current quota and price only for a selected, authorized design.
9. **Storage:** Replication supports defined copies and can preserve unwanted changes; it is not automatically an independent historical backup. Check versioning, delete-marker behavior, retention/immutability, account/key access and recovery-point age. Restore must connect the right photo to the right booking and prove authorized retrieval.
10. **Visibility:** A trace helps locate time and errors across instrumented portions of an observed request. Sampling and missing instrumentation limit coverage; correlation is not proof of cause. Combine customer completion/latency, accepted-versus-completed backlog age, dependency signals and a controlled reproduction. For new tracing instrumentation, account for AWS's OpenTelemetry direction and X-Ray SDK/daemon lifecycle.

## Transfer challenge

Peak demand exceeds the assumed AZ capacity and the recovery Region's applied worker quota. Route 53 caching means some customers continue using the prior answer. The design cannot claim full service at peak. A credible response prioritizes or sheds work according to the business promise, communicates degraded behavior, preserves authoritative write control, and reconciles uncertain requests. It names the exact capacity/quota change and routing observation needed before recovery. It does not assume changing DNS instantly moves every client or that an infrastructure success metric proves a correct booking.

No AWS service is configured or tested by this assessment. It proves only whether a learner can explain the supplied fictional facts; independent learner response and technical rubric calibration remain pending.
